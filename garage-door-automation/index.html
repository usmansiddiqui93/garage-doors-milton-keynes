Can you automate an existing garage door?

Very often, yes. If your current door is in good condition, there is no need to replace it just to go electric. We automate existing garage door setups across Milton Keynes by fitting a quality motor, or opener, to the door you already have. You get the same press-a-button convenience as a new electric door, usually for a fraction of the cost and with far less disruption.

The key requirement is that the door itself is sound and well balanced. A motor is designed to guide a door that is already easy to move, not to haul a heavy or sticking one. That is why every conversion starts with a check of the door's condition.

Which doors can be converted to electric?

Up and over doors

Both canopy and retractable up and over garage doors can usually be automated. A ceiling-mounted operator drives a carriage along a rail, which is connected to the top of the door by an arm. Retractable doors, which run fully back on horizontal tracks, are the most straightforward. Canopy doors, which stick out over the drive when open, need a special bow-arm or canopy conversion kit so the door follows its natural arc. We check the springs and pivots first and replace any worn parts, because an out-of-balance door will burn out a motor early.

Sectional doors

Many manual sectional doors were designed with automation in mind and convert very neatly. The operator is fixed to the ceiling and connects to a bracket on the top panel. Because a sectional door's torsion springs do most of the lifting, a correctly balanced door puts little strain on the motor.

Roller doors

Manual roller doors use springs inside the barrel to help you lift the curtain. Converting one normally means replacing that spring barrel arrangement with a tubular motor and adding a control unit and safety edge. On some doors this works well; on older or lightweight curtains it can be better value to fit a new electric roller garage door. We will tell you honestly which route makes sense after we have seen it.

Side hinged doors

Side hinged doors can be automated with a pair of arm or ram operators mounted inside the garage. This needs enough clearance around the frame and a firm structure to fix to, so it is assessed on a case-by-case basis.

Remotes and controls

Every conversion comes with remote handsets, and you can add more for other drivers in the household. Modern systems use rolling-code or encrypted signals, which change every time you press the button and are much harder to copy than the fixed codes on many older openers. Optional extras include:

  • Wall-mounted push buttons inside the garage or house
  • Wireless keypads, so you can let family in without a remote
  • Key switches for an external override
  • Smartphone control and status alerts on compatible systems
  • Receivers that work with car-integrated buttons on some models

Safety features we include

A motorised door must stop and reverse if it meets an obstruction. Safety is not an add-on, so every installation is set up and tested before we leave:

  • Force limitation and auto-reverse – the motor senses resistance and reverses if the door touches something while closing.
  • Photocell safety beams – recommended on most doors and essential on many, these stop the door before it reaches a person, pet or car.
  • Safety edges – pressure-sensitive strips on the bottom of roller doors.
  • Soft start and soft stop – gentler on the door and quieter in use.
  • Manual release – an internal cord or handle, plus an external release if the garage has no other entrance.
  • Automatic locking – many operators hold the door down when closed, making it harder to lever up.

Battery backup for power cuts

A power cut can leave your car stuck in the garage. Many operators can be fitted with a battery backup unit that takes over automatically when the mains supply fails and runs the door for a limited number of cycles. It is a sensible option if the garage is your main way in or out, or if you would struggle to use the manual release. The battery will need replacing every few years, and we check it during a routine service.

Power supply and installation

The operator needs a fused spur near the ceiling where the motor will sit. If your garage already has power, this is usually simple; if not, a qualified electrician will need to provide one first. A typical conversion takes a few hours. We fit the rail or motor, connect the door, set the limits and force, install safety devices, code your remotes and show you how everything works.

How much does it cost to automate a garage door?

The cost depends on the door type, the motor and any extras such as photocells, keypads or a battery backup. Converting a well-balanced sectional or retractable door is usually the most affordable; canopy doors and roller conversions cost a little more. Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Can any garage door be made electric?

Most up and over, sectional and side hinged doors in good condition can be automated, and many manual roller doors too. The door must open and close easily by hand and be properly balanced. If it is heavy, rusted or distorted, we will recommend repairs first, or a new electric door if that is better value.

How long does it take to fit a garage door motor?

A typical conversion takes a few hours, provided there is a suitable power supply near the ceiling. Canopy doors that need a conversion arm, roller doors that need a new tubular motor, or doors that need spring or roller work first can take longer. We confirm the timing when we quote.

What happens to my electric garage door in a power cut?

Without power the motor will not run, but you can still open the door using the manual release. If the garage has no other entrance, we fit an external release too. For extra peace of mind, a battery backup unit can keep the door working for a limited number of cycles until the power returns.

Are electric garage doors more secure?

They can be. Many operators hold the door firmly closed and resist being levered up, and modern rolling-code remotes are much harder to copy than old fixed-code systems. Security still depends on the door itself, so we check its condition and can advise on improving locks or adding a stronger door.

Can I add extra remotes or a keypad later?

Yes. Additional handsets can be coded to your motor at any time, and wireless keypads or push buttons can usually be added without new wiring. Let us know the make and model of your operator and we can bring compatible remotes or a keypad on our next visit.
