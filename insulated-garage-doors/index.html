Insulated garage doors in Milton Keynes are worth a serious look if your garage is attached to the house, sits under a bedroom or doubles as a gym, office or utility room. An old single-skin steel door is often the biggest cold spot in the whole building. Swap it for an insulated door with proper seals and the garage stays noticeably warmer, drier and quieter.

We supply and fit insulated roller, sectional, side hinged and up and over doors. Below is an honest guide to how much difference each type makes and what you'll pay.

Why insulate your garage door?

Heat moves out through the door itself and through the gaps around it. A thin steel panel conducts cold straight through, and a door with worn or missing seals lets wind, rain and leaves in at the bottom and sides. An insulated door tackles both problems.

  • Warmer adjoining rooms. Rooms above or next to an integral garage often feel colder; reducing heat loss through the door helps.
  • Less condensation. Warmer internal surfaces mean less condensation dripping onto cars, tools and stored boxes.
  • A usable space. Workshops, home gyms and utility areas become comfortable for more of the year.
  • Quieter operation. Foam-filled panels deaden noise from the door and from the street.
  • Better rigidity. Double-skinned panels are stiffer and less prone to dents than single-skin steel.

Will it pay for itself in heating bills? That depends on whether you heat the garage and how it connects to the house. Our guide are insulated garage doors worth it? looks at the real-world benefits in more depth.

How insulated doors compare

Thermal performance is usually expressed as a U-value in W/m²K; the lower the number, the less heat escapes. Manufacturers quote figures in different ways (some for the panel alone, some for the whole fitted door), so use the table below as a rough comparison rather than a spec sheet.

Door typeTypical constructionRelative insulation
Single-skin steel up and overThin painted steel sheetPoor
Insulated up and overFoam-backed or double-skin panelFair
Foam-filled rollerAluminium laths with foam coreGood
Insulated side hingedDouble-skin leaves with compression sealsGood to very good
Insulated sectionalAround 40–42mm foam-filled steel panelsVery good
Thermally broken sectionalThicker panels and thermal breaks in frameBest available

As a broad guide, a good insulated sectional door can perform several times better than a single-skin steel door. Glazing and pedestrian doors reduce performance slightly, though double-glazed units help.

Your insulated door options

Insulated sectional garage doors

The top choice where warmth matters most. Thick foam-filled panels, seals at the head, sides, base and between every panel, and they open vertically too. Details on our sectional garage doors page.

Insulated roller garage doors

Foam-filled aluminium laths give a good level of insulation in a space-saving package. Some manufacturers offer thicker, enhanced-insulation laths for a further improvement. Ideal where ceiling space must stay clear; see roller garage doors.

Insulated side hinged garage doors

Because the leaves press shut against a rebated frame, a well-made insulated side hinged door can be very draught-tight. A good choice for workshops where you'll be walking in and out all day.

Insulated up and over doors

The budget route. Insulation is modest, but a foam-backed panel and new brush seals make a real difference compared with an old single-skin door.

Colours, windows and automation

Insulation doesn't restrict style. You can choose from the same wide palette of colours, woodgrain finishes and panel designs, and add windows in insulated or double-glazed units. Most insulated doors can be electric; a motor also helps keep the door firmly closed against its seals.

Security

Double-skinned panels are harder to cut or dent than a single sheet, and sectional and roller doors with a motor resist being levered up. Ask us about security-tested options if your garage holds bikes, tools or a valuable car.

Insulated electric garage doors

Pairing insulation with a motor is a popular combination. An operator pulls the door firmly onto its seals every time, so you don't get the small gaps that appear when a manual door isn't shut quite fully. If you're weighing up automation at the same time, our electric garage doors page covers remotes, keypads and safety features.

A note on garage conversions

If you're turning the garage into a habitable room, the rules change. Building Regulations then apply to the new wall, window or door that replaces the garage door, with minimum insulation standards to meet, so it's worth speaking to your local building control team early. An insulated garage door is the right answer when the space is still a garage that you simply want to be warmer.

Who benefits most from an insulated door?

  • Integral garages sharing walls or a ceiling with living space, a common layout on many newer Milton Keynes homes.
  • Converted or part-converted garages used as gyms, studios, offices or laundry rooms.
  • Workshops where you'll spend long periods working.
  • Garages storing temperature-sensitive items such as paint, electronics or a classic car.

If your garage is detached and used only for the car, a well-sealed standard door may be all you need. We'll tell you honestly which makes sense.

Fitting an insulated door properly

An insulated door only performs if it's fitted to seal. At survey we check that the opening is square, the floor at the threshold is level and the reveals are sound. Uneven floors are common, and a gap of even 10mm under the door undoes much of the benefit, so we'll recommend a threshold seal or floor-level correction where needed.

On fitting day we remove the old door, install the frame or guides, hang the door, then fine-tune the seals so they compress evenly all round. If you have persistent damp or condensation, our guide on condensation and weather seals explains other steps that help.

Keeping insulated doors in good shape

Seals are the part most likely to wear. We replace worn brush, rubber and bottom seals, repair motors and adjust doors that no longer close tightly. An annual garage door service includes a seal check.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Which type of garage door is the best insulated?

Insulated sectional doors with thick foam-filled steel panels generally offer the best thermal performance, especially models with thermally broken frames. Insulated side hinged doors and foam-filled roller doors come next. Insulated up and over doors give a useful improvement over single-skin steel but can't match a sectional. Good seals matter as much as the panel itself.

Can I insulate my existing garage door instead?

You can fit insulation kits to the back of some up and over doors, but results are limited because the gaps around the door remain and the extra weight can unbalance the springs. New seals often give a bigger improvement. If your door is old, replacing it with an insulated model is usually the more effective and longer-lasting solution.

Will an insulated garage door stop condensation?

It will usually reduce it, because the inside face of the door stays warmer. Condensation also depends on ventilation and moisture sources in the garage, such as a wet car or a tumble dryer. Combining an insulated door with good seals and some ventilation tends to give the best result.

Can insulated garage doors have windows?

Yes. Most insulated sectional, roller and side hinged doors can be ordered with windows, typically in double-glazed or insulated acrylic units. Windows reduce the overall insulation slightly compared with a solid door, but the effect is small if the glazed area is modest. We can show you window options at survey.
