Modern garage doors that lift the look of your home

Modern garage doors can change the whole face of a house. On many homes the garage door is the single biggest surface on the front elevation, so swapping a tired white up and over for a sleek, flush anthracite grey sectional makes an instant difference. We supply and fit contemporary doors across Milton Keynes and the surrounding Buckinghamshire, Bedfordshire and Northamptonshire towns, and colour is usually the first thing customers want to talk about.

This page covers the main contemporary styles, the most popular garage door colours and finishes, and how to choose a combination that suits your home rather than just following a trend.

Contemporary garage door styles

Flush and smooth panels

A completely flat face with no ribs or grooves is the cleanest modern look. It works especially well on new builds with render, cladding or large-format windows. Smooth finishes show dirt a little more readily, but a quick wash keeps them crisp.

Horizontal ribs and lines

Wide or narrow horizontal ribs are the most popular contemporary choice on sectional garage doors. They emphasise width, sit nicely with modern brickwork and hide minor marks better than a flush face.

Vertical cladding effects

Some ranges imitate vertical timber cladding or board-on-board designs, ideal for homes with timber or composite cladding elsewhere on the elevation.

Glazed and full-vision designs

Horizontal strips of glass or full aluminium-framed glazing create a striking architectural look. See our page on garage doors with windows for glazing types and privacy options.

Modern side hinged and roller doors

Contemporary doesn't have to mean sectional. Modern side hinged garage doors in flush composite or aluminium suit homes with low headroom, while black or grey roller garage doors give a minimalist look with no visible tracks under the ceiling. Our aluminium garage doors page covers both.

The most popular garage door colours

Anthracite grey (RAL 7016)

Anthracite grey has been the colour of the decade for windows, doors and garage doors, and it's the most requested modern finish we quote across Buckinghamshire. It's a deep, slightly blue-tinged charcoal that pairs with grey or black window frames, red or buff brick and white render. Many sectional and roller ranges now offer anthracite grey at little or no extra cost.

Black

Black garage doors — often in RAL 9005 jet black or a softer black-grey — look bold and premium, especially with glazing or on lighter brick. Matt and textured finishes hide fingerprints and look more refined than gloss. Dark colours absorb more heat in direct sun, so quality manufacturers design their dark finishes accordingly.

White and cream

White garage doors are still the most common colour in the UK and remain a smart, clean choice, especially where windows and fascias are white. Cream and off-white shades are a softer alternative on traditional homes.

Other greys

Lighter greys such as slate, basalt or window grey give a modern feel without being as dark as anthracite. Grey composite garage doors and grey roller shutter doors are both widely available.

Blue, green, red and brown

Steel blue, dark green, burgundy and brown remain popular, particularly on traditional and rural properties. Deep greens and blues are enjoying a revival on period homes and cottages. Most manufacturers offer a range of RAL colours, sometimes at extra cost.

Woodgrain finishes

Woodgrain foils and printed finishes in golden oak, rosewood, walnut or grey oak give the warmth of timber with the low maintenance of steel or composite. Real wooden garage doors remain an option if you want the genuine article and don't mind upkeep.

How to choose a colour that works

  • Match the window frames. A garage door in the same colour as your windows and front door looks deliberate and cohesive.
  • Consider the brick. Anthracite and black contrast well with buff and red brick; white can look stark against very dark brick.
  • Think about the light. Colours look different on a south-facing drive than in a sample book — ask to see samples outdoors.
  • Check your estate's rules. Some newer developments have covenants or management rules about external colours.
  • Choose texture deliberately. Smooth, fine-grain and woodgrain textures change how a colour reads.

Pros and cons of modern finishes

On the plus side, factory-applied colours on steel and aluminium are durable and need only occasional washing, and a modern door can noticeably improve kerb appeal. On the downside, special RAL colours and premium textures can add to the price and lead time, and very dark finishes can show dust and pollen on a flat face.

Can you repaint an existing door instead?

Sometimes. A sound steel or timber door can be repainted, and a professional finish can freshen it up for a fraction of the cost of a new door. However, it won't change the style, insulation or mechanism. Our guide to painting garage doors explains what's realistic, and our bespoke garage doors page covers one-off designs if you want something truly unique.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the most popular garage door colour in the UK?

White is still the most common colour overall because it has been the standard for decades. For new and replacement modern doors, though, anthracite grey (RAL 7016) has become the most requested choice, followed by black and other greys. Woodgrain finishes remain popular on more traditional homes. We'll bring samples so you can compare them against your brickwork.

Is anthracite grey a good colour for a garage door?

For most modern homes, yes. Anthracite grey pairs well with grey or black window frames, red or buff brick and render, and it hides everyday dirt better than white. It's widely available on sectional, roller and side hinged doors, often at no extra cost. If your windows are white, consider whether a lighter grey or white would look more cohesive.

Do black garage doors get hot?

Dark colours absorb more heat from the sun than light ones, so a black door facing south can get warm to the touch. Reputable manufacturers design their dark finishes and materials with this in mind. On some plastic or composite doors, very dark colours may carry specific guidance, which we'll explain when you choose your door.

Can I get a modern garage door in a woodgrain finish?

Yes. Many steel sectional, up and over and composite doors come with woodgrain foils or printed finishes in shades such as golden oak, walnut and grey oak. They give a warm, natural look without the maintenance of real timber. Flush and ribbed modern designs both work well with woodgrain, especially alongside timber cladding.

Are modern garage doors more expensive?

Not necessarily. Many contemporary sectional and roller doors in anthracite grey or white cost much the same as traditional designs. Prices rise for special RAL colours, premium textures, large glazed areas and flush aluminium architectural doors. Our garage door price guide gives typical figures, and we'll confirm your exact cost after a free survey.
