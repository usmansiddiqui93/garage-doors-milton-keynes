Electric garage doors in Milton Keynes take the hassle out of getting the car in and out, especially on dark, wet winter evenings. Press a button on your remote as you turn into the drive and the door is open by the time you arrive. We supply and install new automatic garage doors of every type, and we can often motorise the door you already have.

This page explains the different ways a garage door can be automated, the features worth paying for and what an electric door typically costs.

Which garage doors can be electric?

Almost any modern garage door can be automated. The type of door decides the type of motor.

Door typeHow it's motorisedGood to know
RollerTubular motor hidden inside the barrelNeat and compact; motor comes as standard on most models
SectionalBelt or chain operator on the ceilingVery smooth and quiet; anti-lift locking on many operators
Up and over (retractable)Ceiling-mounted operator with a drive armIdeal candidate for automating an existing door
Up and over (canopy)Ceiling operator with a canopy arm kitDoor must be well balanced first
Side hingedPair of arm or ram operatorsNeeds clearance either side and inside

Electric roller garage doors are the most popular choice we fit, because the motor is concealed and the door takes up no room on the drive or ceiling. For full details on that style, see our roller garage doors page, or sectional garage doors if insulation is your priority.

The benefits of an automatic garage door

  • Convenience: no getting out of the car in the rain, no heavy lifting.
  • Accessibility: a real help for anyone with limited mobility, back problems or reduced grip strength.
  • Security: the motor holds the door shut, and modern remotes use rolling codes that change every time you press the button.
  • Safety: soft start and stop, obstacle detection and safety edges stop and reverse the door if it meets resistance.
  • Kinder on the door: a motor opens and closes the door at a controlled speed, which reduces wear compared with a door that's yanked open.

And the downsides?

Automation adds cost and introduces electrical parts that may eventually need repair. You'll need a power supply in the garage, and in a power cut you'll use the manual release. For most people those are small trade-offs for the everyday convenience.

Features and options to consider

Remote controls and access

Two remote handsets are usually included. Popular extras are additional handsets, a wireless keypad outside the door, an internal wall button and a key switch. Smartphone control via Wi-Fi lets you open the door for a delivery or check it's closed while you're at work, and many systems now integrate with voice assistants and smart home hubs.

Safety devices

Photocells across the opening stop the door if a person, pet or car breaks the beam. A pressure-sensitive safety edge on the bottom of the door does the same on contact. Garage doors sold in the UK should comply with EN 13241, the European product standard for industrial, commercial and garage doors, which covers safe operation including force limits.

Battery backup and manual release

A battery backup keeps the door working for a limited number of cycles in a power cut. Every electric door also has a manual release. If the garage has no other entrance, an external emergency release lets you get in.

Insulation and finishes

Automation doesn't limit your choice of look. Electric doors come in the same wide range of colours, woodgrains, panel styles and windows as manual doors, and insulated electric garage doors are popular for integral garages. Our insulated garage doors page explains the options.

Lighting

Many operators include a courtesy light that switches on as the door opens and turns off after a few minutes.

Automating your existing door

If your current door is sound, well balanced and runs freely by hand, adding a motor can be far cheaper than buying a whole new door. Retractable up and over doors and manual sectional doors are usually the best candidates. Canopy doors can be done with the right arm, but if springs are worn they'll need attention first.

We won't automate a door that isn't safe to automate. If the springs are tired, the frame is twisted or the panel is badly dented, we'll explain why and give you the option of a repair or a new door. Our garage door automation service page covers retrofit kits in more depth.

Who are electric doors right for?

  • Anyone who uses the garage daily for the car.
  • Households with older or less mobile members.
  • Families who want to keep bikes and tools secure but easy to reach.
  • Owners of integral garages who want the internal door and the garage door to work together smoothly.

What happens on installation day

We'll have already surveyed the garage, checked the power supply and agreed where the motor and controls will go. A fused spur close to the motor is needed; if there isn't one, we'll advise you on getting it in place before we arrive.

On the day, we fit the door (if it's new), mount the operator, set the open and closed positions and the force settings, install photocells or a safety edge, and pair every remote and keypad. Before we leave, we test the safety reversal with you and show you the manual release.

Electric door repairs and servicing

Remotes stop working, motors fail, limits drift and photocells get knocked out of line. We repair electric doors and operators from all major makes. Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Can any garage door be made electric?

Most can. Roller, sectional and retractable up and over doors are all straightforward to automate. Canopy doors need a special arm and must be well balanced, and side hinged doors use arm operators that need clearance. Very old or damaged doors may not be safe to motorise, in which case we'll explain the options and costs honestly.

What happens to an electric garage door in a power cut?

Every motorised door we fit has a manual release so it can be opened by hand from inside. Many operators also accept a battery backup that keeps the door working for a set number of cycles. If the garage is your only way in, we can fit an external emergency release so you're not locked out.

Are remote control garage doors secure?

Modern remotes use rolling code technology, which changes the signal each time you press the button so it can't simply be recorded and replayed. When the door is closed, the motor and drive also hold it in place, and many sectional operators add anti-lift locking. Keep spare handsets safe and remove lost ones from the system.

How long does it take to fit an electric garage door?

A new single electric roller or sectional door is usually fitted in about half a day to a full day. Automating an existing door typically takes two to four hours. We'll give you a realistic time on your quote and make sure the power supply is ready beforehand to avoid delays.

Do you install automatic garage doors in Northampton?

Yes. As well as covering all of Milton Keynes, we regularly install automatic garage doors in Northampton, Bedford and Aylesbury. You'll get the same free survey, written quote and installation by our own fitters, wherever you are in our coverage area.
