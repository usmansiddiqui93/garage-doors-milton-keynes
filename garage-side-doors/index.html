Garage side doors in Milton Keynes for everyday access

Garage side doors in Milton Keynes are one of those small upgrades that make a big difference. A pedestrian door at the side or rear of the garage lets you grab the lawnmower, wheel out a bike or get to the freezer without opening the main door every time. It also gives a second way in and out, which is handy if the main door is electric and you're caught by a power cut.

Side doors are often neglected, though. Many we see are original timber doors with a single cheap lock, swollen frames and rotten bottom rails — and because they're usually tucked out of sight, they can be the weakest point in the garage's security. Replacing one is a quick, affordable job that's usually done in a few hours.

Personnel doors, pedestrian doors and wicket doors: what's the difference?

You'll see a few different names, and they overlap:

  • Garage side door / pedestrian door / personnel door: a separate, full-height single door set into its own opening, usually in the side or back wall of the garage.
  • Garage door with a pedestrian door built in: also called a wicket door — a smaller door within the main garage door itself, found on some up and over and sectional designs, and common on timber side hinged doors where one leaf acts as the walk-through door.
  • Side-by-side pedestrian door: a matching personnel door fitted next to the main garage door on the front elevation, often with a matching frame and finish.

Garage side door materials

Steel garage side doors

Steel pedestrian doors are tough, secure and good value. Many manufacturers make steel personnel doors to match their up and over and sectional ranges, so the door at the side can share the same colour and texture as the main door. Insulated steel doorsets are available if the garage is heated. Find matching main doors on our steel garage doors page.

uPVC garage side doors

The most common replacement we fit. uPVC side doors are weatherproof, need no painting and often come with multi-point locks. Half-glazed or solid-panel versions are available, and they can be colour-matched to your house windows. See composite and uPVC garage doors for matching main doors.

Composite garage side doors

Thicker and more solid than uPVC, composite doors feel like a front door and generally offer strong security and good insulation. A great choice if the garage holds valuable tools, bikes or a home gym.

Wooden garage side doors

Timber pedestrian doors suit traditional homes and timber main doors, and can be made to measure for non-standard openings. Ledged and braced, framed, or with glazed top lights — all are possible. They do need periodic painting or staining.

Glazed or solid?

Garage side doors with windows bring daylight into a dark garage and make it easier to see who's outside. Solid doors are more private and slightly more secure. A good middle ground is a door with a small, high glazed panel in obscure laminated glass. If you'd rather not have a separate door at all, side hinged garage doors with an unequal split give you a narrow walk-through leaf within the main opening.

Pros and cons of adding or upgrading a side door

  • Pros: everyday access without opening the main door, a safe second exit, better ventilation, and an opportunity to fix a security weak spot.
  • Cons: a brand-new opening means building work and possibly building control involvement, and another door is another thing to lock at night.

Security for pedestrian garage doors

Because side doors are often hidden from the street, they deserve proper locks. Look for:

  • Multi-point locking that engages hooks or bolts at several points down the frame.
  • Anti-snap cylinders rather than basic euro cylinders.
  • Strong frames and hinges, with hinge bolts or dog bolts on outward-opening doors.
  • Laminated or toughened glass in any glazed panel.

Some pedestrian doorsets are tested to PAS 24, a British standard for enhanced security performance of doors. If the garage connects directly into your house, the door between the two matters most of all. Our secure garage doors page explains these standards in plain English, and our guide to securing your garage has more practical tips.

Who needs a garage side door?

  • Households who use the garage for storage, a utility area or a workshop and are in and out all day.
  • Anyone with an electric main door who wants a manual way in.
  • Homes with an old, draughty or insecure side door that's past repair.
  • Garages being converted into a gym, office or hobby room — see garage conversion doors.

How we fit a new side door

We measure the opening and check the condition of the lintel, reveals and threshold. Old timber frames often hide rot or loose brickwork, so we look closely before we quote. On the day, we cut out the old frame, fix the new doorset plumb and square, seal it to the brickwork, fit the threshold and adjust the locks so they engage smoothly. Most replacements are done in half a day.

Tip: if you're replacing the main garage door too, do both together. You'll get matching colours and finishes, and often a better overall price than doing the jobs separately.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

How much does a garage side door cost?

As a guide, a steel pedestrian door fitted in an existing opening usually costs from about £700, uPVC from around £850 and composite from about £1,100, with timber somewhere in between depending on the design. Creating a new opening in a wall costs more because it involves building work. We confirm a fixed price in writing after a free survey.

Can you fit a garage door with a pedestrian door in it?

Yes. Some up and over and sectional ranges offer an integrated pedestrian or wicket door, and timber side hinged doors can be made with one leaf acting as the walk-through door. Built-in wicket doors usually have a raised threshold, so they're not ideal for wheeling heavy items. We'll show you the options that suit your opening.

Do I need permission to add a side door to my garage?

Replacing an existing side door is usually straightforward. Forming a brand-new opening involves structural work, such as fitting a lintel, and may need building regulations approval, and planning rules can apply on listed buildings or in conservation areas. It's best to check with your local council first. We can fit the doorset once the opening has been formed.

What is the most secure type of garage side door?

A solid composite or steel doorset with a multi-point lock, anti-snap cylinder and a strong, well-fixed frame is typically the most secure choice. Doors tested to PAS 24 have been independently assessed for resistance to attack. Good installation matters as much as the door itself, because a strong door in a weak frame is easily defeated.

Can my new side door match my main garage door?

In most cases, yes. Many manufacturers produce pedestrian doors in the same colours, textures and panel styles as their main garage doors, and uPVC and composite doors come in a wide range of colours and woodgrain foils. If we're replacing both together, we'll specify them as a matching set.
