Can Garage Doors Be Repaired? Repair vs Replace Explained

Which garage door faults are worth fixing, when replacement makes more sense, and what each option typically costs.

  • Clear signs to replace

Can garage doors be repaired? Yes, in most cases. Broken springs and cables, worn locks and handles, noisy rollers, misaligned tracks, faulty motors, remotes and photocells, and even damaged panels or slats can usually be fixed for a fraction of the cost of a new door. Replacement makes more sense when the door itself is failing: badly rusted steel, rotten timber, widespread impact damage, parts that are no longer made, or repair bills that keep coming back. A useful rule of thumb is that if a repair costs more than about half the price of a comparable new door, or the door is near the end of its life, replacement is usually the better long-term choice.

This guide explains which faults are worth repairing, the signs that it's time for a new door, and typical costs for each, so you can make an informed decision.

Garage door faults that can usually be repaired

Most garage door problems come down to wear on moving parts, and those parts can be replaced individually.

  • Springs and cables: the most common repair. Springs fatigue over time and cables fray. Both can be replaced, but they're under high tension, so this is always a job for a professional. See our garage door spring and cable repairs page.
  • Locks and handles: replacement locks, cylinders, rods and latches are available for most makes.
  • Rollers, wheels and tracks: worn rollers can be replaced and bent tracks straightened or renewed.
  • Motors, remotes and safety features: remotes can be replaced and reprogrammed, photocells swapped, and control boards or entire motors replaced. Our electric garage door repairs service covers all major makes.
  • Panels and slats: damaged roller door slats can often be replaced individually, and some sectional panels can be replaced if the door is a current model.
  • Seals and weatherstrips: worn seals are cheap to replace and make a real difference to draughts and water ingress.

Signs it's time to replace your garage door

Sometimes repairing just delays the inevitable. Replacement is often the better choice if:

  • steel panels are rusting through, especially along the bottom edge
  • timber is soft, rotten or splitting, particularly around hinges and locks
  • the door has been hit by a vehicle and the frame or several panels are distorted
  • parts are obsolete and the manufacturer no longer supports the model
  • you've had several repairs in a short period and more are needed
  • security is poor, such as a thin single-skin door with a basic lock
  • the garage is cold, damp or noisy and you'd benefit from an insulated garage door

Age matters too. Our guide on how long garage doors last gives typical lifespans by door type, which can help you judge whether a repair is worth it.

The 50% rule and other ways to decide

There's no single right answer, but these questions help:

  1. How much will the repair cost compared with a new door? If it's more than around half, replacement usually gives better value.
  2. How old is the door? Spending a few hundred pounds on a 25-year-old door that's rusting at the base is rarely worthwhile; the same repair on a five-year-old door is an easy decision.
  3. Is it a one-off fault or a pattern? One broken spring is normal wear. Springs, cables, rollers and the lock all failing within a year suggests the whole door is wearing out.
  4. Is the door doing what you need? If you want better security, insulation or electric operation, a new door might give you all of that at once.
  5. Are parts available? Some older or discontinued doors are hard to source parts for, which can make repairs slower or impossible.
Honest advice: We'd always rather repair a door that has plenty of life left than sell you a new one you don't need. If replacement is the better option, we'll explain why and give you clear written prices for both, so you can choose.

Can garage doors be repaired after accidental damage?

Reversing into a garage door is more common than people like to admit. Whether the door can be repaired depends on where the damage is. If only the skin of the door is dented and it still opens, closes and locks smoothly, it can often be left alone or have a single slat or panel replaced. If the tracks, frame, arms or bottom rail have been bent, the door may bind, run unevenly or put strain on the springs and motor, which can become a safety issue.

After any impact, stop using an electric door until it's been checked, and don't try to force a manual door that's sticking. Take photos before anything is moved, as your insurer may ask for them, and ask for a written report and quote so you have the details for a claim.

Repairing electric garage doors

Electric doors have more components, so there's more that can go wrong, but also more that can be fixed. Many issues are simple: flat remote batteries, dirty photocells or limits that need resetting. Our guide on what to do when your electric garage door is not working walks you through the checks. Motors themselves can be replaced without replacing the door, and an older manual door in good condition can often be fitted with a motor through our garage door automation service rather than being replaced.

Repair options by door type

Up and over doors

Springs, cables, cones, arms, locks and rollers are all replaceable. The usual reason to replace is rust or a distorted panel after an impact.

Roller doors

Slats, end caps, guides, motors, remotes and safety edges can be replaced. A curtain that's badly damaged across many slats may be better replaced as a whole.

Sectional doors

Torsion springs, cables, rollers, hinges, motors and seals are all repairable. Panels can be replaced if the model is still in production.

Side hinged doors

Hinges, locks, shoot bolts and seals are straightforward. Timber can sometimes be repaired, but extensive rot usually means a new door.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Can a garage door be repaired or does it need replacing?

Most garage doors can be repaired. Springs, cables, locks, rollers, motors, remotes and even individual slats or panels can usually be replaced. Replacement is usually better if the door is rusted through, rotten, badly damaged by an impact, very old, or needs repeated repairs. An engineer can inspect it and give you prices for both options.

Is it worth repairing an old garage door?

It depends on the door's condition and the cost of the repair. A sound old door with one broken spring is usually worth fixing. If the door is rusting, rotting or has needed several repairs recently, or the repair would cost more than about half a new door, replacement normally gives better value and better security.

Can a dented garage door be repaired?

Small dents usually don't affect how the door works and can often be left. On roller doors, damaged slats can usually be replaced individually. On sectional doors, a damaged panel can sometimes be replaced if matching panels are still made. Larger impacts that distort the frame, tracks or several panels often mean a replacement door is more practical.

How much does a garage door repair cost?

As an indicative UK guide, simple adjustments start from around £80–£150, spring replacements are typically £120–£300, and motor replacements £350–£750. The exact price depends on the door, the fault and the parts needed. We always give you a clear price before starting any work, and there's no obligation to go ahead.

Can you repair garage doors from any manufacturer?

We repair all major makes of garage door, including up and over, roller, sectional and side hinged doors, plus electric motors and remotes. For some older or discontinued models parts can be harder to find, but compatible alternatives are often available. If a door genuinely can't be repaired economically, we'll explain why and offer replacement options.
