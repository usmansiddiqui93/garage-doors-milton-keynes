Does Home Insurance Cover Garage Doors?

General information on how UK home insurance usually treats garage doors and what's kept in the garage — so you know which questions to ask your insurer.

  • Common exclusions to check

In most cases, yes — UK home insurance usually covers a garage door under the buildings part of a policy, but only for specific insured events such as storm, fire, flood, vandalism, impact or damage caused during a break-in. It typically does not cover a door that has simply worn out, rusted or broken through age, such as a snapped spring or a tired motor. Items stored inside the garage fall under contents cover, often with lower limits for outbuildings and conditions about how the garage is locked. Every policy is different, so treat this guide as general information only and always check your own policy wording or speak to your insurer or broker.

Important: we are garage door specialists, not insurance advisers. This page explains how policies commonly work in general terms. It is not financial or insurance advice, and your own cover may differ. Your policy documents and your insurer have the final word.

Buildings insurance vs contents insurance

The first thing to understand is which part of your policy applies.

Buildings insurance: the garage and its door

Buildings insurance covers the structure of your home and its permanent fixtures and fittings. A garage — whether integral, attached or detached — is often included, and the garage door is usually treated as part of that structure. Some policies list detached garages and outbuildings separately or apply different limits, so check how yours describes them.

Contents insurance: what you keep in the garage

Contents insurance covers your belongings. Tools, bikes, garden machinery, freezers and sports kit stored in a garage are normally covered under contents, but many policies set a lower limit for items kept in garages and outbuildings than for items in the house. Bicycles, high-value tools and garden machinery may also have their own limits or need to be specified.

Accidental damage cover

Accidental damage is sudden, unexpected and unintended damage that is not caused by one of the standard insured events. Reversing the car into your own garage door is the classic example. Many standard buildings policies do not include accidental damage automatically; it is often an optional extra or included only on higher tiers. Where it is included, it may still have exclusions or a higher excess. Depending on the circumstances, your car insurance may also be relevant, so it is worth checking both.

Wear and tear: the most common exclusion

Almost every home insurance policy excludes damage caused by gradual deterioration, wear and tear, rust, rot, lack of maintenance, and mechanical or electrical breakdown. For garage doors, that typically means these are not covered:

  • A broken torsion or tension spring from normal use.
  • A frayed or snapped lifting cable.
  • A motor or control board that fails through age.
  • Rust holes, rotten timber or a seized lock.
  • Damage that has developed slowly, such as a door gradually dropping out of alignment.

Insurers may also look at the condition of the door when assessing a storm claim. If a door was already corroded or poorly maintained, they may argue the weather simply exposed an existing problem. Keeping records of servicing and repairs can help show the door was in reasonable condition. Our garage door maintenance guide and garage door servicing page explain how to keep a door in good order.

Theft from garages and security requirements

Garages are a common target because they often hold valuable bikes and tools, and they are frequently less secure than the house. Insurers know this, and policies often include specific conditions.

Common conditions to look for

  • Forcible and violent entry: some policies only pay for theft from a garage or outbuilding if there are visible signs of forced entry.
  • Locks: the policy may expect garage and side doors to be locked when not in use, and some specify types of lock.
  • Outbuilding limits: a maximum payout for items kept outside the main house.
  • Integral vs detached: an integral garage with a connecting door to the house may be treated differently from a detached one.
  • Bicycles and high-value items: these may need to be listed, or may need extra security such as a ground anchor.

A secure, well-maintained garage door lowers the chance of a break-in in the first place. For high-security options, see our secure garage doors range.

Tip: if you fit a new, more secure door or add an alarm, it is worth telling your insurer. It will not always change your premium, but it keeps your details accurate and in some cases may help.

Does home insurance cover electric garage door repairs?

Usually only if the damage was caused by an insured event. For example, a motor damaged by a lightning strike, a flood or a fire may be covered, depending on the policy. A motor or remote that simply stops working through age or an internal fault is normally treated as mechanical or electrical breakdown and excluded. Some home emergency add-ons cover certain urgent situations, such as a garage door that will not close and leaves the home insecure, but limits and definitions vary widely. Check your policy schedule to see whether you have home emergency cover and what it includes.

How to make a garage door insurance claim

  1. Make the door safe and secure as far as you safely can. Do not try to deal with damaged springs or cables yourself; they are under high tension and dangerous.
  2. Take photos of the damage, inside and out, before anything is moved or repaired.
  3. Report crimes to the police and note the crime reference number if there has been a break-in or vandalism.
  4. Contact your insurer or broker as soon as possible and ask how they want you to proceed.
  5. Get a written quote for the repair or replacement. Many insurers ask for one, and some appoint their own contractors.
  6. Keep damaged parts until the insurer confirms they are not needed.
  7. Keep receipts for any emergency work needed to secure the property.

When we assess a damaged door, we can provide a clear written quote describing the damage and the work needed, which you can pass to your insurer. We repair and replace all major makes; see our garage door repairs and garage door replacement pages.

Is it worth claiming?

Before you claim, compare the cost of the repair with your excess and think about the possible effect on future premiums and any no-claims discount. For a small repair, paying directly is sometimes the better option; for storm damage or a door wrecked in a break-in, a claim may make more sense. This is a personal decision, and your insurer or broker can explain how a claim might affect you.

Questions to ask your insurer

  • Is my garage (and its door) included in my buildings cover, and are there separate limits for it?
  • What is the limit for contents kept in the garage or outbuildings?
  • Do I have accidental damage cover for the buildings?
  • Are there lock or security requirements for the garage and side door?
  • Does theft from the garage require signs of forced entry?
  • Do I have home emergency cover, and does it include garage doors?

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Does home insurance cover a broken garage door spring?

Usually not. Springs wear out with use, so a spring that snaps through age is normally treated as wear and tear or mechanical breakdown, which most policies exclude. If the spring was damaged by an insured event, such as a break-in or impact, the position may be different. Check your policy wording, and remember that springs are dangerous and should be repaired by a professional, such as our spring and cable repairs team.

Am I covered if I reverse into my own garage door?

It depends on your policy. Standard buildings cover often does not include accidental damage, so you may only be covered if you have accidental damage cover as an add-on or on a higher-tier policy. Your car insurance may also be relevant depending on your level of motor cover. Check both policies, or ask your insurer or broker, before arranging the repair.

Is my detached garage covered by home insurance?

Many buildings policies include detached garages and outbuildings, but some apply separate limits or conditions, and contents kept inside may have a lower limit than items in the house. The policy may also have security requirements, such as locks on the garage and side door. Look for sections on outbuildings in your policy documents, or ask your insurer to confirm.

Do insurers require a certain type of garage door lock?

Some policies do set security conditions for garages, particularly where valuable items are stored, while others simply expect doors to be locked when not in use. Requirements vary between insurers and policies. If you are unsure, ask your insurer what they expect. Fitting a more secure door or extra locks can also reduce the risk of a break-in in the first place.

Will you provide a quote for my insurance claim?

Yes. If your garage door has been damaged, we can inspect it and provide a clear written quote describing the damage and the repair or replacement needed, which you can pass to your insurer.
