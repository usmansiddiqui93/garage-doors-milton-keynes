What is a roller garage door?

A roller door is a curtain of horizontal slats (usually called laths) that runs up and down in two vertical guide rails. When it opens, the curtain wraps around a barrel inside a box at the top of the opening. Nothing swings out onto the drive and nothing hangs from the garage ceiling, which is why roller doors are so popular on short driveways and in garages used for storage.

Most modern domestic roller doors use double-skinned aluminium laths filled with foam. The foam adds rigidity, quietens the door and gives a useful amount of insulation. Single-skin steel laths are still available for budget or utility installations, but for a home in Milton Keynes the foam-filled aluminium version is what we fit most often. If you want to know more about the mechanics, our guide on how roller garage doors work covers the barrel, springs, motor and manual override in detail.

Roller shutter garage doors: the pros and cons

Why people choose them

  • Space saving. The door goes straight up, so you can park right up to it and still open it.
  • Clear ceiling. Unlike a sectional or retractable door there are no overhead tracks, so you can fit shelving or roof storage.
  • Easy to automate. Most roller doors are electric as standard, with a tubular motor hidden in the barrel.
  • Fits awkward openings. Because each door is made to measure, odd widths and heights are not a problem.
  • Weather resistance. Brush seals in the guides and a rubber bottom seal keep out most wind-blown rain and leaves.

Things to be aware of

  • Headroom for the box. You need space above the opening or behind the lintel for the coil, typically between 200mm and 300mm depending on the door height.
  • Looks. A roller door is clean and modern rather than traditional. It suits most estates but may not be right for a period cottage.
  • Insulation is good, not the best. Foam-filled laths beat single-skin steel easily, but a 40mm insulated sectional panel performs better again.
  • Repairs need a specialist. Motors, limit settings and curtain alignment are not DIY jobs.

Options for your roller door

Lath size and material

Wider laths (around 77mm) are the usual choice for standard domestic doors; slimmer laths roll into a smaller box and suit tight headroom. Extruded aluminium laths are available on high-security models and are noticeably more rigid than roll-formed ones.

Colours and finishes

White, anthracite grey and black roller garage doors are the big sellers at the moment, but most manufacturers offer a wide palette of standard colours plus woodgrain-effect finishes such as golden oak and rosewood. You can match the guides and box to the curtain or pick a contrasting colour to tie in with your windows and front door.

Insulation

All the foam-filled roller doors we fit offer some insulation. If you use the garage as a gym, workshop or utility room, ask us about insulated roller garage doors with thicker laths and upgraded seals. For a full comparison of insulated doors, see our page on insulated garage doors.

Automation and control

Electric roller garage doors are now the norm. Typical options include extra remote handsets, a wall-mounted keypad, smartphone control, an internal push button and a safety edge with obstacle detection. Every motorised door we fit has a manual override so you can still get in during a power cut. Our electric garage doors page explains the different operator types.

Security

A good roller door is hard to lever up because the curtain is locked into the guides and held down by the motor. For extra peace of mind some models are tested to LPS 1175, the Loss Prevention Standard that rates how long a product resists a determined attack with specified tools. We cover what that means in practice in our guide are roller garage doors secure?

Who are roller doors best for?

Roller doors suit you if any of these sound familiar:

  • Your driveway is short and an up and over door would hit the car when it opens.
  • You want to use the garage roof space or fit a ceiling rack.
  • You would like an electric door without the cost of a sectional.
  • You are replacing a tired up and over door and want something that runs quietly and seals better.

They are less suited to garages with very low lintels and no space for an external box, or to listed and conservation-area properties where a traditional look is expected. In those cases side hinged garage doors are often a better fit.

What's involved in fitting a roller door

  1. Survey. We measure the width, height, headroom and side room, check the condition of the brickwork and lintel, and look at where the power supply will come from.
  2. Made-to-measure order. Your door is manufactured to your sizes and colour. Lead times vary by manufacturer and finish; we give you a realistic estimate with your quote.
  3. Removal. On the day we take out the old door and frame and take it away for recycling.
  4. Installation. The guides go in first, then the box and barrel, then the curtain. Most single doors are fitted in around half a day.
  5. Setting up. We set the open and closed limits, test the safety edge, pair your remotes and show you how to use the manual override.

The motor needs a fused spur near the box. If there isn't one, we can advise on getting one installed before fitting day.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

How much headroom does a roller garage door need?

Most domestic roller doors need between 200mm and 300mm above the opening for the box that the curtain coils into. Taller doors need a bigger box. If you don't have that space inside, the box can often be fitted on the outside face of the wall instead. We measure this on the free survey and recommend the best fixing position.

Can I open an electric roller door in a power cut?

Yes. Every motorised roller door we fit has a manual override, usually a winding handle or a release cord inside the garage. Some motors also accept a battery backup that keeps the door working for a limited number of cycles. If your garage has no other way in, ask us about an external override key so you are never locked out.

Are roller doors noisy?

A well-fitted foam-filled aluminium roller door is fairly quiet, with a low hum from the motor and a soft rolling sound from the curtain. Noise usually increases when guides are dirty, fixings have loosened or the curtain is out of alignment. A regular service keeps things running smoothly and quietly.

Do you fit roller shutter garage doors in Northampton and Bedford?

Yes. Although we are based around Milton Keynes, we regularly supply and fit roller shutter garage doors in Northampton, Bedford, Aylesbury and the towns in between. The process is the same wherever you are: a free survey, a clear written quote and installation by our own fitters, usually completed in about half a day for a single door.

Can a roller door be fitted behind my existing frame?

Usually, yes. Most roller doors are fitted behind the opening, with the guides fixed to the inside of the brickwork and the box tucked behind the lintel. This hides the mechanism and keeps the front of the house tidy. Where side room or headroom is tight we can fit the door within the opening or on the face of the wall instead.
