Garage door spring replacement done safely

Springs are the hidden workhorses of most garage doors. They counterbalance the weight of the door so it feels light to lift and stays put when open. When one breaks, the door becomes heavy, lopsided or impossible to move. Our engineers carry out garage door spring replacement and cable repairs across Milton Keynes on up and over and sectional doors of all major makes, and we carry common springs and cables on the van.

Safety warning: springs and cables on a garage door store a great deal of energy. Releasing or winding them without the correct tools and training can cause serious injury. Please do not attempt to adjust, tension or replace them yourself. This page explains how they work and the warning signs, not how to work on them.

Torsion springs vs tension springs

There are two main types of springs for garage doors in the UK, and knowing which one you have helps us bring the right parts.

Tension springs

Tension (or extension) springs stretch as the door moves. On a typical canopy up and over door you will see a pair of long coil springs either side of the frame, hooked between the door arm and a bracket. Older Henderson, Garador and many unbranded doors use this layout. Because they stretch and relax every cycle, tension springs fatigue over time and tend to fail without warning.

Torsion springs

Torsion springs twist rather than stretch. They are wound around a shaft above the opening on sectional doors, and on many retractable and some canopy up and over doors from makers such as Cardale and Hörmann. As the door closes, the spring winds up; as it opens, it unwinds and helps lift the door through cables and drums. Torsion springs are held under constant load, which is why adjusting them needs specialist winding bars and training.

Cones and cables explained

On a torsion-spring door, each spring has a cone at each end. One cone is fixed to a bracket, the other grips the shaft. The cones are what hold the spring's wound tension, and if a set screw loosens or a cone cracks, the spring can let go suddenly. People often refer to this as a "cones and cables" fault.

The cables run from drums on the shaft down to the bottom corners of the door. On up and over doors they connect the spring mechanism to the door frame. Cables for up and over garage doors wear at the point where they pass over pulleys or drums, then fray and eventually snap, usually on one side first. That is why the door suddenly opens at an angle.

Signs your springs or cables are failing

  • A loud bang from the garage, often when nobody is using the door
  • The door feels much heavier than usual, or will not stay open
  • One side of the door lifts higher than the other
  • A visible gap in a coil spring, or a spring hanging loose
  • Frayed, kinked or rusty cables, or a cable that has come off its drum
  • Squealing, grinding or jerky movement as the door travels
  • An electric opener straining, stopping part-way or reversing

If you notice any of these, stop using the door. Running a door on one spring or one cable puts the frame, tracks and any motor under heavy strain, and can bend parts that would otherwise have been fine.

What our spring and cable repair includes

  1. Make the door safe. We secure the door before any work so it cannot drop.
  2. Identify the mechanism. We match spring size, wire gauge, length and winding direction, or cable length and end fittings, to your door's make and weight.
  3. Replace in pairs. Where a door has two springs or two cables, we normally replace both. The surviving one is the same age and usually close to failure, and matched parts keep the door level.
  4. Rebalance and adjust. We set the tension so the door lifts smoothly and holds its position, then check the rollers, pivots and track.
  5. Test. The door is cycled several times, and on electric doors we re-check the motor's force and limit settings.

Replacement springs for up and over garage doors

Up and over doors account for most of the spring jobs we do in Milton Keynes, because so many of the town's garages still have their original canopy or retractable doors. Springs are matched to the door's weight, so a heavy timber or insulated door needs a different spring from a lightweight steel one. Fitting the wrong rating leaves the door either crashing down or flying up. Read more about the door type on our up and over garage doors page, or see makes such as Cardale garage doors, whose cable replacement is a frequent request.

Why springs break more often in winter

Customers are often surprised that broken springs cluster in the colder months. Low temperatures make spring steel slightly more brittle, and condensation inside an unheated garage encourages surface rust, which creates weak points along the coils. A spring that has been fatiguing for years will often give up on the first frosty morning. Keeping the springs clean and dry, and having them checked during a service, will not make them last forever, but it does reduce the chance of being caught out on the way to work.

Spring replacement cost

The price depends on the spring type, the door's make and whether cables or cones need renewing at the same time. A pair of tension springs on a standard single door is at the lower end; torsion springs on a double sectional door take longer and the parts cost more. Typical guide ranges are listed on our garage door prices page, and we confirm the exact cost before starting.

Help keeping springs healthy

Springs have a finite number of cycles, but dirt, rust and an unbalanced door shorten their life. Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Can I replace garage door springs myself?

We strongly advise against it. Garage door springs, especially torsion springs, are held under high tension and can release that energy violently. Without the correct winding bars, parts and experience, there is a real risk of serious injury and damage to the door. A professional replacement is quick and the door is properly rebalanced afterwards.

Why should both springs be replaced at the same time?

Springs on the same door are the same age and have done the same number of cycles, so when one breaks the other is usually close behind. Replacing them as a matched pair keeps the door level, avoids a second call-out soon after and ensures both sides lift with equal force.

How do I know if my door has torsion or tension springs?

Look above and around the door frame. A single shaft running across the top of the opening with coiled springs wrapped around it means torsion springs. Long springs stretched along the sides of the frame, attached to arms, are tension springs. A photo sent when you call helps us confirm and bring the right parts.

How long do garage door springs last?

It varies with the spring quality, door weight and how often the door is used. A door opened several times a day will wear its springs out much sooner than one used occasionally. Rust and poor balance shorten spring life, which is why a regular service that checks spring condition is worthwhile.

Is it safe to use the door with a broken cable?

No. With one cable gone, the door lifts unevenly and can jam, twist its frame or drop suddenly. If you have an electric opener, disconnecting it stops anyone operating the door by accident. Leave the door where it is and call us to replace the cables and check the rest of the mechanism.
