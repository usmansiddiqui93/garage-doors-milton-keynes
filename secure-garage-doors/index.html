Secure garage doors in Milton Keynes: why it matters

Secure garage doors in Milton Keynes protect more than the car. Garages hold bikes, power tools, golf clubs and garden machinery worth thousands of pounds, and on many homes an integral garage leads straight into the house. Yet the garage door is often the weakest door on the property — an old up and over with a single centre handle lock, or a timber side door with a rotten frame, can be opened far more easily than most people realise.

The good news is that improving garage security doesn't always mean a new door. Sometimes the answer is a lock upgrade or extra bolts; sometimes it's time for a door built to a recognised security standard. We'll look at your garage honestly and explain both.

Which garage doors are most secure?

Roller garage doors

A well-made electric roller door with interlocking slats, deep guide rails and an anti-lift mechanism is one of the most secure domestic options, because there's no handle or lock to attack from outside and the motor holds the curtain down. Some roller doors have been independently tested to LPS 1175. For the detail, read our guide are roller garage doors secure? or see the range on our roller garage doors page.

Sectional garage doors

Modern electric sectional doors are generally secure, with steel panels and motors that include anti-lift protection or locking in the drive rail. Thick insulated panels are hard to force, and there's no external lock to pick.

Steel up and over doors

A good steel up and over is reasonably strong, but the standard centre lock is its weak point. Many manufacturers offer upgraded multi-point locking, which bolts the door at the sides or corners as well. Find out more on our steel garage doors page.

Side hinged doors

Side hinged doors can be very secure with multi-point locks, drop bolts top and bottom, and hinge protection. Quality depends heavily on the frame and fixings.

Garage side doors

Pedestrian doors are often forgotten. A solid composite or steel doorset with a multi-point lock makes a huge difference — see our garage side doors page.

Security standards explained

You'll see a few standards mentioned in brochures. Here's what they mean in plain English. They describe how a product has been tested; they don't on their own guarantee a door can't be broken into, and they apply to the specific product as tested and installed.

LPS 1175

LPS 1175 is a Loss Prevention Standard published by the Loss Prevention Certification Board (LPCB), part of BRE. It tests how long a door, shutter or barrier resists a physical attack using defined sets of tools. Older issues grade products from SR1 upwards; the newer issue uses a letter for the tool set and a number for the minutes of delay, such as A1 or B3. For homes, the lower ratings are typical; higher ratings are mainly used on commercial buildings. Certified products are listed in the LPCB's Red Book, so you can check a claim yourself.

Secured by Design

Secured by Design (SBD) is the official UK police security initiative. Products that meet its "Police Preferred Specification" can display SBD accreditation, which usually means they've been tested to recognised standards and come from a manufacturer with independent certification. The accreditation belongs to the product and its maker, not to whoever installs it.

PAS 24

PAS 24 is a British standard for the enhanced security performance of doorsets and windows. It's most relevant to pedestrian doors, including the door between an integral garage and the house. In England, building regulations Approved Document Q expects doors giving access into a new dwelling — including from an integral garage — to meet PAS 24 or an equivalent standard. It's a sensible benchmark for any replacement side or internal garage door.

Sold Secure

Sold Secure tests locks and add-on security products such as garage door defenders and ground anchors, grading them bronze, silver, gold or diamond.

Garage door locks and upgrades

If your door is in good condition, these upgrades can make it much harder to force:

  • Replacement locks: swapping a worn or basic up and over lock for a better-quality or multi-point version.
  • Garage door defenders: a heavy, padlocked steel bar fixed into the ground outside the door. Very visible and a strong deterrent.
  • Internal bolts: shoot bolts or padbolts on the inside, ideal where the garage is also reachable from the house.
  • Anti-lift and motor locking: on electric doors, making sure anti-lift protection is fitted and working.
  • Manual release protection: on some electric doors, the emergency release can be shielded so it can't be hooked from outside.

Our step-by-step guide to securing your garage covers lighting, alarms and habits as well as hardware.

Are electric garage doors secure?

Generally, yes. A motor that holds the door closed, combined with anti-lift features, makes most electric roller and sectional doors hard to force. Modern remotes use rolling or encrypted codes, which makes cloning far harder than with old fixed-code transmitters. If you still have an older system, we can advise on upgrading the receiver and remotes.

Who should prioritise security?

  • Homes with an integral garage that opens into the house.
  • Anyone storing bikes, motorbikes, tools or sports equipment.
  • Garages that can't be seen from the house or the street.
  • Households asked by their insurer about garage locks — see does home insurance cover garage doors?

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the most secure type of garage door?

For most homes, a well-made electric roller or sectional door with anti-lift protection is the most secure option, because there's no external lock to attack and the motor holds the door shut. Roller doors tested to LPS 1175 offer independently verified attack resistance. Side hinged and up and over doors can also be secure with multi-point locks.

Are roller garage doors secure?

Good-quality roller doors are very secure, especially electric models with interlocking slats, deep guides and anti-lift devices. Cheap single-skin doors are weaker, so the specification matters. Some ranges are certified to LPS 1175. Our guide are roller garage doors secure? explains what to look for when comparing doors.

Can you fit better locks to my existing garage door?

In many cases, yes. Up and over doors can often take an upgraded lock or multi-point locking kit, side hinged doors can have extra bolts and better cylinders, and almost any door can be protected with a ground-fixed garage defender. If the door itself is badly worn or damaged, though, new locks won't fix the weak point, and we'll tell you.

Do you fit Secured by Design garage doors?

We can supply and fit doors from manufacturers whose products carry Secured by Design accreditation or LPS 1175 certification, where those products suit your opening. The accreditation belongs to the product and its manufacturer rather than to us as installers. We'll show you the relevant product details so you can check the rating for yourself.

Does PAS 24 apply to garage doors?

PAS 24 is mainly relevant to pedestrian doorsets, such as a garage side door or the door from an integral garage into the house. In England, Approved Document Q expects doors providing access into new homes, including from an integral garage, to meet PAS 24 or equivalent. Main up and over, roller or sectional doors are usually assessed against other standards.
