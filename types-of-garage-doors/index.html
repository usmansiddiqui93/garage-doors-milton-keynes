Types of Garage Doors in the UK: A Plain-English Guide

Up and over, sectional, roller, side hinged or sliding? Here is how each type of garage door works, what it costs and which kind of garage it suits best.

There are five main types of garage doors in the UK: up and over doors, sectional doors, roller doors, side hinged doors and, less commonly, side sliding doors. Up and over doors are the traditional British choice and usually the cheapest. Sectional and roller doors lift vertically, keep the drive clear and are usually insulated and electric. Side hinged doors open like a pair of large doors and suit workshops and period homes. The right one depends on your garage's space, how you use it and your budget.

Below we explain how each type works, what it is good and less good at, and where it makes sense. At the end there is a quick comparison table with indicative prices.

The main types of garage doors in the UK

1. Up and over garage doors

An up and over door is a single rigid panel that tilts outwards at the bottom and slides up into the garage roof space. It is the door you see on most British garages built from the 1960s onwards, and it is still the most affordable option for a like-for-like replacement. There are two main types of up and over garage doors:

  • Canopy: the door pivots on arms and, when open, about a third of it sticks out beyond the front of the garage, forming a canopy. Canopy gear is simple and needs less space inside the garage.
  • Retractable: the door runs on horizontal tracks and slides fully back into the garage, so nothing protrudes when it is open. Retractable doors are usually smoother and easier to automate, but need more space inside.

Both types swing outwards as they open, so you need to leave room in front of the door before opening it. Most are steel, but GRP and timber versions exist. See our up and over garage doors page for styles and prices.

2. Sectional garage doors

A sectional door is made of horizontal panels (usually four or five) joined by hinges. It lifts straight up, and the panels follow curved tracks to sit flat under the garage ceiling. Because it never swings out, you can park right up to it, and because it is sealed all the way round, it is one of the best types for insulation and draught-proofing. Most sectional doors have foam-filled steel panels and are electrically operated. They do need some headroom above the opening and clear ceiling space for the tracks. Read more on sectional garage doors.

3. Roller garage doors

A roller door is made of narrow slats (usually foam-filled aluminium) that coil around a barrel inside a box above the opening. It takes up almost no space inside or outside the garage, which makes it ideal for short driveways, garages used for storage and openings where a sectional's ceiling tracks would get in the way. Different types of roller garage doors include standard insulated domestic doors, higher-security models tested to standards such as LPS 1175, and heavier-duty steel roller shutters. Nearly all are electric, with a manual override for power cuts. Our roller garage doors page covers the options.

4. Side hinged garage doors

Side hinged doors are the oldest style: two leaves (or sometimes an unequal pair) hung on hinges and opening outwards like a pair of French doors. They leave the full opening and the ceiling completely clear, and you can open just one leaf to walk in, which makes them popular for garages used as workshops or for bikes. They come in timber, steel, GRP and composite, and they suit period properties well. The trade-off is that you need space in front to swing the doors open. See side hinged garage doors.

5. Side sliding garage doors

Less common in the UK, a side sliding door is a sectional door that runs sideways along the inside wall of the garage rather than up to the ceiling. It suits garages with very little headroom or where the ceiling is needed, and it allows quick pedestrian access by opening it partially. You need a clear side wall at least as long as the door is wide.

Types of electric and automatic garage doors

Any of the types above can be electric. Sectional and roller doors are almost always motorised, while up and over and side hinged doors can be bought electric or automated later. The main types of garage door openers are:

  • Ceiling-mounted (boom) operators: a motor in the garage roof drives a rail or chain, used on sectional and up and over doors.
  • Tubular or barrel motors: built into the barrel of a roller door, hidden inside the coil box.
  • Arm operators: fitted to side hinged doors, one per leaf, much like an electric gate.

Modern openers have soft start and stop, safety reversal if the door meets an obstruction, and remote control, with some offering app control. Learn more about electric garage doors.

Thinking of motorising an old door? An operator should never be used to overcome a badly balanced door. Springs and cables need to be in good condition first, and tensioned springs should only be adjusted by a professional.

Types of springs on garage doors

Most garage doors use springs to counterbalance their weight, so a heavy door feels light to lift. Up and over doors use either tension (extension) springs on canopy gear or springs and cables on retractable gear, while sectional doors usually use torsion springs on a shaft above the opening, or sometimes tension springs at the sides. Roller doors often rely on springs inside the barrel or on the motor itself. Whatever the type, springs store a lot of energy and can cause serious injury, so replacement is always a job for a trained engineer.

Types of garage door materials

Door type and material are separate choices. Most types come in more than one material:

  • Steel: the most common, strong and cost-effective, used for up and over and sectional doors.
  • Aluminium: light and rust-resistant, used for roller slats and some sectionals.
  • Timber: traditional and attractive, needs regular finishing.
  • GRP (fibreglass): low maintenance, can mimic woodgrain.
  • Composite: layered materials for strength, insulation and low upkeep.

Our guide what are garage doors made of? compares each material in detail.

Types of locks for garage doors

Up and over doors usually have a central handle-operated lock that throws bars into the frame, which can be supplemented with extra bolts or rim locks. Sectional and roller doors are usually locked by the operator itself, with some including anti-lift devices that resist the door being forced upwards. Side hinged doors use multipoint or mortice locks plus top and bottom bolts on the fixed leaf. For tips on improving security, see how to secure a garage door.

Types of double garage doors

For a double garage, you can choose one wide door or two single doors. A single wide sectional or roller door gives an uninterrupted opening, while two singles keep the central pier and let you open just one side. Wide up and over doors are available too, though they are heavy and benefit from automation. Check standard widths and heights in our garage door sizes UK guide.

Which type of garage door is best for you?

A few simple questions narrow it down quickly:

  1. Do you park close to the door, or is the drive short? Choose a sectional or roller door so nothing swings out.
  2. Is the garage a workshop, gym or utility space? An insulated sectional or roller door will keep it warmer and drier.
  3. Is headroom or ceiling space limited? A roller door or side hinged door is usually the answer.
  4. Is budget the priority? A steel up and over door offers the best value.
  5. Do you want a traditional look? Timber side hinged or timber-effect GRP doors suit older properties.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What are the 5 types of garage doors?

The five main types of garage doors in the UK are up and over, sectional, roller, side hinged and side sliding. Up and over doors tilt up into the roof space, sectional doors lift in panels under the ceiling, roller doors coil into a box above the opening, side hinged doors open like a pair of doors, and sliding doors run along a side wall.

What is the most common type of garage door in the UK?

The steel up and over door is still the most common type on UK homes, particularly on houses built from the 1960s to the 1990s. However, insulated sectional and roller doors have become increasingly popular for new installations, because they keep the driveway clear, seal better and are usually supplied with an electric operator.

Which type of garage door takes up the least space?

A roller garage door takes up the least space overall. It coils into a compact box above the opening, so it needs no room in front of the garage and leaves the ceiling clear. Side sliding doors are another option for very low headroom. Sectional doors keep the drive clear but run back under the ceiling.

Which type of garage door is best for insulation?

Insulated sectional doors generally perform best, because they have thick foam-filled panels and seals on all four sides. Insulated roller doors are also good, particularly with a quality bottom seal. Single-skin steel up and over doors offer little insulation. Our guide are insulated garage doors worth it? explains when it really matters.

Can any type of garage door be made electric?

Most can. Sectional and roller doors are usually supplied electric, and up and over and side hinged doors can be automated with the right type of operator. The door must be in good condition and properly balanced first. We check this at survey and can fit an operator to many existing doors in Milton Keynes and nearby.
