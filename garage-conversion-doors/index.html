Garage Conversion Doors: French Doors, Bifolds & Alternatives

Your options for replacing a garage door in a conversion, the rules to check first, and cheaper alternatives if you keep the garage.

  • French, bifold and wall options
  • Planning and regulations explained
  • Insulated alternatives if keeping

The main options for garage conversion doors are to replace the garage door with French doors, bifold doors or sliding patio doors, or to brick up the opening with a new wall and a window. French and bifold doors bring in lots of light and suit garden rooms, studios and playrooms; a wall and window is the most common choice when the garage becomes a bedroom, office or living space facing the street. Whichever you choose, Building Regulations approval is usually needed when a garage becomes a habitable room, and some homes need planning permission, so check with your local council (for most of our area, Milton Keynes City Council) before you start. If you're keeping the garage as a garage, an insulated garage door may give you much of the comfort you're after for far less cost.

This guide compares the options, explains the regulations to be aware of, gives rough costs, and covers the alternatives if you decide not to convert.

Replacing a garage door with French doors

Replacing a garage door with French doors is popular where the garage faces a garden, or where the converted room will be a hobby room or garden-facing lounge. A typical single garage opening is around 7ft to 8ft wide (see our garage door sizes UK guide), which is wider than most standard French door sets. That means the opening is usually reduced with a dwarf wall, side panels or fixed side windows, or the doors are made to measure to suit.

Points to consider when you convert a garage door to French doors:

  • Threshold: garage floors are often lower than the house and slope towards the door. The floor usually needs raising and a proper threshold detail to keep water out.
  • Frame and lintel: the existing lintel should be checked, and the new door frame properly fixed and sealed into the opening.
  • Security: choose doors with multi-point locking, ideally tested to PAS 24.
  • Material: uPVC French doors for a garage are budget-friendly, aluminium gives slim sightlines, and timber suits period homes.

Bifold doors for garage conversions

Bifold doors fold back in panels to open most of the width. In a garage conversion with bifold doors, they work best on a rear or side garage leading to a patio. Aluminium bifold garage doors are the most common choice because the frames are strong and slim; timber bifolds look lovely but need more maintenance. You'll need a level, well-drained threshold and enough room inside or outside for the folded panels.

Bifold garage doors that keep the garage

You may also see "bifold garage doors" used for timber garage doors that fold to one side while the building stays a garage. These are a niche, usually made-to-measure product. If you like the look, side hinged garage doors and wooden garage doors give a similar traditional appearance, can include glazing, and are easier to source. Automating folding doors is possible but specialist and relatively costly.

Bricking up with a wall and window

For many street-facing conversions, the neatest result is to remove the garage door, build a new wall in the opening and fit a window. This makes the room feel like part of the house and is often the most practical layout for a bedroom or office. A new wall usually needs a foundation, because the area under the old garage door often doesn't have one deep enough. Matching bricks and mortar colour makes a big difference to how it looks from the street.

Planning permission and Building Regulations

Check before you start: Rules depend on your home and its planning history. Contact Milton Keynes City Council's planning and building control teams, or your own local council if you're outside Milton Keynes, before committing to any conversion work.

Planning permission

Many garage conversions don't need planning permission, but some do. It's more likely to be needed where:

  • permitted development rights have been removed by a condition on the original planning consent, which is common on newer estates
  • the property is in a conservation area or is a listed building
  • the work changes the outside of the building significantly or extends it
  • the garage is a separate building, or the home is a flat or maisonette

Some estates also have restrictive covenants or rules about parking spaces, so check your deeds as well.

Building Regulations

Turning a garage into a habitable room is normally treated as a change that needs Building Regulations approval. Building control will typically look at:

  • foundations for any new wall in the old door opening
  • damp-proofing and raising the floor
  • insulation to walls, floor and ceiling, and the thermal performance of new windows and doors
  • ventilation of the new room
  • fire safety, including escape routes and windows where required, and fire separation if part of the space remains a garage
  • structural changes, such as removing a central pier

Getting approval and a completion certificate also matters when you come to sell.

Converting two garage doors into one

If you have a double garage with two single doors, you might want one wide door instead. To convert two garage doors into one, the central pier is removed and a steel beam or correctly sized lintel is installed, designed by a structural engineer and approved by building control. It's a building job first; once the new opening is formed, a single wide double garage door can be fitted.

Rough costs for garage door conversion work

As a very broad UK guide, replacing a garage door with French doors typically costs somewhere in the region of £2,000–£5,000 including framing, threshold and making good; bifold doors can be £4,000–£9,000 or more depending on width and material; and a new infill wall with a window is often around £2,500–£6,000. A full conversion into a habitable room usually runs well into five figures once insulation, flooring, electrics, heating and decoration are included. Converting two garage doors into one typically costs several thousand pounds for the structural work alone, plus the new door. These are rough figures only; get firm, written quotes after a survey.

We specialise in garage doors and garage side doors rather than full conversions, so for conversion building work you'll want a builder, and building control. We're happy to advise on the garage door side of things and remove the old door if needed.

Keeping the garage? Consider an insulated door

Lots of people looking into conversions really want a warmer, drier, more usable garage for a home gym, workshop or storage. If you want to keep parking or storage, an insulated garage door can make a big difference without planning or a change of use. Our guide are insulated garage doors worth it? explains the benefits, and our guide to garage door weather seals and condensation covers draughts and damp. Want more natural light? Garage doors with windows are another option, and a new garage side door can make a garage much easier to use day to day.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Can you replace a garage door with French doors?

Yes. The garage door is removed and French doors are fitted into the opening, usually with side panels, fixed windows or a dwarf wall to fill the extra width. The floor often needs raising and a proper threshold fitting to keep water out. If the garage becomes a habitable room, Building Regulations approval is usually needed, so check with your council first.

Do I need planning permission to replace my garage door with a window or French doors?

Often not, but sometimes yes. Planning permission may be needed if permitted development rights were removed when your estate was built, if you're in a conservation area or a listed building, or if the work significantly changes the outside. Building Regulations usually apply to a conversion regardless. Check with Milton Keynes City Council or your local council before starting.

How much does it cost to replace a garage door with French doors?

As a rough UK guide, replacing a garage door with French doors often costs around £2,000–£5,000 including framing, threshold and making good, depending on materials and size. Aluminium or made-to-measure doors cost more. A full conversion into a habitable room costs considerably more once insulation, heating and electrics are added. Always get written quotes after a survey.

What is the cheapest way to make a garage usable without converting it?

Fitting an insulated garage door, adding good weather seals, and improving ventilation is usually the most cost-effective way to make a garage warmer and drier. It doesn't involve a change of use, so planning and Building Regulations are rarely an issue. Adding a secure side door or a glazed garage door can also make the space far more usable.

Can two single garage doors be converted into one double door?

Yes, but it's structural work. The central pier is removed and a steel beam or lintel is installed, designed by a structural engineer and approved by building control. Once the new opening is formed, a single wide double garage door can be fitted. Expect several thousand pounds for the structural work alone, plus the cost of the new door.
