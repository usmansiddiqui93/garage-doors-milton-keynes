Steel garage doors in Milton Keynes account for more of the doors we fit than any other material, and for good reason. Steel is strong, comparatively light, takes a durable factory finish in almost any colour and gives you the widest choice of styles at the most sensible prices. We supply and fit steel up and over, sectional, roller and side hinged doors, and we repair and repaint older metal doors too.

What makes a good steel garage door?

Not all steel doors are equal. The differences that matter are mostly hidden under the paint.

  • Galvanising. A zinc coating on both faces of the steel is the main defence against rust. Good doors are galvanised before they're painted.
  • Coating system. Polyester powder-coated or coil-coated paint finishes, applied in the factory, are far tougher than anything painted on site. Some premium finishes use thicker plastisol-type coatings.
  • Steel gauge. Thicker steel resists dents and flexes less in wind.
  • Single or double skin. Single-skin panels are the budget option; double-skin panels with a foam core are stiffer, quieter and insulated.
  • Bracing and gear. Reinforcing ribs and good-quality springs and hinges make the door feel solid and last longer.

Types of steel garage door we fit

Steel up and over doors

The most common garage door in the UK and the best-value route to a new frontage. Available on canopy or retractable gear in dozens of designs. Our up and over garage doors page explains the two types of gear.

Steel sectional doors

Double-skinned, foam-filled steel panels give strength and insulation in a door that opens vertically. They're the go-to choice for integral garages.

Steel roller doors

Steel roller shutter garage doors use roll-formed steel laths and are a robust, cost-effective option, especially for workshops and outbuildings. For homes, foam-filled aluminium laths are more common, but steel remains popular where toughness matters most.

Steel side hinged doors

Side hinged metal garage doors combine a traditional opening with a slim, modern look and very little maintenance. Insulated double-skin versions are available.

Steel side doors

Matching steel personnel doors let you walk into the garage from the garden without opening the main door.

Colours, finishes and styles

Steel gives you the biggest palette of any material. White is still the standard, but black metal garage doors and anthracite grey are now hugely popular on contemporary homes. You'll also find blues, greens, reds, creams and dozens of RAL colours, plus woodgrain-effect foils and embossed textures that look surprisingly convincing from the street.

Style options include vertical ribs, horizontal ribs, Georgian and cathedral panels, flush smooth panels for a minimalist look, and designs with windows or stainless steel applications.

Insulation, security and automation

Insulation

A single-skin steel door does little to keep heat in. If the garage adjoins the house, choose a double-skin, foam-filled steel door, or see our insulated garage doors page for the best performing options.

Security

Steel is inherently tough, but the lock and gear make the biggest difference. Look for multi-point locking, anti-lift protection on electric doors and reinforced frames. For a closer look at the standards and features that matter, see our secure garage doors page.

Automation

Steel doors are lighter than timber or GRP, which makes them ideal for automating. Steel retractable up and over doors and steel sectional doors take a ceiling-mounted operator easily, and steel roller doors are usually electric as standard.

Who should choose steel?

  • Homeowners wanting a new door at a sensible price, with minimal maintenance.
  • Anyone after a modern colour such as anthracite or black.
  • Owners planning to automate, now or later.
  • Landlords who want a durable, low-upkeep door across several properties.

If you'd prefer something that won't dent and has a timber look, compare GRP garage doors.

What fitting involves

Steel doors are usually the quickest to install. After a survey to measure the opening and check the brickwork, the door is ordered in your chosen size and colour. On the day our fitters remove the old door and frame, install the new frame square and level, hang the door, set the springs or operator and seal around the frame. Most single steel up and over doors are fitted in a few hours.

We handle steel doors carefully on site, because a scratch through the coating is the main thing that can lead to rust later on.

Steel door repairs, servicing and refinishing

We repair steel doors from all major makes, including Garador steel up and over doors (see our Garador garage doors page). Typical jobs include replacing springs and cables, straightening frames, renewing locks and handles and fixing doors that have been knocked by a car.

If the panel is sound but the finish is tired, repainting can be an option. Metal doors need the right preparation and primer to avoid peeling; our guide can you paint garage doors? covers what works.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Do steel garage doors rust?

A good-quality steel door is galvanised and factory coated, so it resists rust well for many years. Rust usually starts where the coating has been scratched or chipped through to bare metal, or along bottom edges that sit in standing water. Touching up damage promptly and keeping the bottom of the door clean helps prevent it spreading.

Can a dented steel garage door be repaired?

Small dents can sometimes be eased out, but steel panels don't repair invisibly, and a badly creased panel will usually need replacing. On some up and over doors the panel can be replaced on the existing frame; on sectional doors individual panels can sometimes be swapped. We'll assess the damage and give you the most cost-effective option.

What colour steel garage doors are most popular?

White remains a common choice, but anthracite grey and black have become very popular on modern homes, often matched to grey or black windows. Woodgrain-effect finishes such as golden oak and rosewood are also popular on more traditional houses. We can bring colour samples to your survey so you can see them against your brickwork.

Are steel garage doors secure?

Steel is a strong material, but security depends mostly on the lock, frame and fixing. A modern steel door with multi-point locking, a solid frame and, if it's electric, anti-lift protection is a good deterrent. Older steel up and over doors with a worn single lock are more vulnerable. See our guide on how to secure your garage for practical upgrades.
