Can You Paint Garage Doors?

Which garage doors can be painted, the best paint for each material, how to get a finish that lasts, and when a new factory-finished door is the better option.

Yes, you can paint most garage doors — steel, timber, GRP (fibreglass) and even aluminium roller doors can all be painted with the right preparation and the right paint. The secret is matching the primer and topcoat to the material: a direct-to-metal or metal primer plus exterior metal paint for steel, a microporous exterior paint or stain for wood, and a suitable adhesion primer for GRP and aluminium. Good preparation matters more than the brand of paint. The main things to watch are manufacturer warranties, dark colours on some materials, and roller door curtains, which flex as they roll and can crack a thick coat.

Painting garage doors by material

Not sure what your door is made of? A magnet will stick to steel but not to aluminium, GRP or timber, and our guide what are garage doors made of? explains how to tell the materials apart. Repainting at the right time also helps a door reach a full lifespan, as covered in how long do garage doors last?

Painting metal garage doors

Steel garage doors are the most commonly painted, often to refresh a tired white door or switch to a modern colour such as anthracite grey or black. Most have a factory-applied coating over galvanised steel, which gives a good base if it is sound.

What is the best paint for metal garage doors?

Look for an exterior paint designed for metal, either a direct-to-metal (DTM) product or a metal primer followed by an exterior gloss or satin. Many UK homeowners use well-known exterior metal paints from DIY and trade suppliers. Avoid interior paints and standard emulsion; they will not bond or weather properly. On galvanised or bare bright metal, a specific primer for galvanised surfaces is usually needed, as normal paint can peel off zinc.

How to paint metal garage doors

  1. Wash and degrease the whole door with a sugar soap or degreaser and rinse thoroughly.
  2. Deal with rust: wire-brush or sand any rust back to clean metal and apply a rust-inhibiting primer.
  3. Key the surface lightly with a fine abrasive pad so the new paint grips the old coating.
  4. Prime bare areas (or the whole door if the paint system requires it).
  5. Apply two thin coats of exterior metal paint, following the drying times on the tin.
  6. Leave the door to cure before operating it heavily, especially where panels meet.

Mask off seals, handles, locks and any windows, and never paint over the springs, cables, rollers or tracks — paint can stop them working properly. If you are thinking about a new metal door instead, our steel garage doors come in a wide choice of factory colours.

Painting wooden garage doors

Painted wooden garage doors are a classic look on period cottages and many village homes, and timber takes paint or stain very well. Wood moves with the seasons, so the finish needs to be flexible and breathable.

What is the best paint for wooden garage doors in the UK?

A good-quality microporous exterior wood paint or exterior wood stain is the usual choice. Microporous finishes let moisture escape from the timber rather than trapping it under the film, which reduces flaking and rot. Stains show the grain; paints give a solid colour.

How to paint wooden garage doors

  1. Clean the door and let it dry thoroughly.
  2. Sand back loose or flaking paint and lightly sand sound areas.
  3. Fill small cracks with an exterior wood filler and treat any knots.
  4. Prime bare timber with a suitable exterior primer.
  5. Apply two coats of exterior wood paint or stain, paying extra attention to the bottom edge and end grain.
Check before you paint: many new timber doors are supplied with a factory base coat or full finish, and manufacturers often require a specific recoat schedule to keep the warranty valid. Read your door's guidance first. See our wooden garage doors page for doors with factory finishes.

Can you paint fibreglass (GRP) garage doors?

Yes. GRP doors are very low maintenance, but after many years the gelcoat can fade or chalk. To paint one, wash it with a mild detergent, lightly abrade the surface, apply an adhesion primer suitable for GRP, then finish with an exterior topcoat. Some manufacturers advise against very dark colours on GRP because they absorb more heat; check before going black. Our GRP garage doors page shows the woodgrain and colour options available new.

Can you paint roller garage doors?

You can paint roller garage doors, but it is the trickiest job of all. The curtain flexes at every lath joint as it winds on to the barrel, so a thick or brittle coat can crack. Paint can also build up in the joints and guides and affect how smoothly the door runs. If you do paint one:

  • Use an etch or adhesion primer suitable for aluminium.
  • Choose a flexible exterior paint and apply thin coats.
  • Paint with the door fully closed, then let it cure fully before running it.
  • Keep paint off the guide rails, seals and bottom rubber.

Most roller doors are factory finished in a hard-wearing coating. Painting may affect the manufacturer's warranty, so check first. Often the better choice is a new door in your preferred colour; see our roller garage doors page or the aluminium garage doors range.

Garage door paint colours

Colour can transform the front of a house. Popular choices at the moment include:

  • Anthracite grey (often RAL 7016) — matches modern grey windows and front doors.
  • Black — bold and smart, especially on contemporary homes.
  • White — clean and traditional, though it shows dirt.
  • Heritage greens and blues — very popular on painted timber doors.
  • Woodgrain stains — warm and natural for cottages and barn conversions.

Painting garage doors black: what to know

Garage doors painted black look striking, but dark colours absorb far more sunlight. On south-facing doors, that heat can cause expansion and stress in some materials, particularly uPVC, GRP and roller door laths. On steel and timber it is generally fine with the right paint. Some manufacturers specify which colours are suitable for their doors, so check before you commit. For ideas on modern colour schemes, take a look at modern garage doors and colours.

Tips for a finish that lasts

  • Paint on a dry day between about 10°C and 25°C, out of direct midday sun.
  • Thin coats are better than thick ones.
  • Do not skip the primer on bare metal or wood.
  • Wash the door a few times a year to keep the finish looking fresh — our garage door maintenance guide explains how.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the best paint for metal garage doors in the UK?

Use an exterior paint made for metal, either a direct-to-metal product or a metal primer followed by an exterior gloss or satin topcoat. Galvanised or bare bright steel usually needs a specific primer so the paint does not peel. Clean, degrease and key the surface first, and treat any rust. Preparation makes more difference to how long the finish lasts than the choice of brand.

Can you paint electric roller garage doors?

It is possible, but take care. The curtain flexes at every lath joint as it rolls up, so use an adhesion primer and a flexible exterior paint in thin coats, and keep paint off the guides and seals. Painting may affect the manufacturer's warranty, so check first. Often a new roller door in a factory colour is the more durable option.

Will painting my garage door void the warranty?

It can. Many manufacturers of steel, aluminium, GRP and uPVC doors state that painting over the factory finish may affect the warranty, and some specify which colours are permitted. Timber doors often come with a required recoat schedule instead. Always check your door's warranty terms or contact the manufacturer before painting, particularly if the door is less than ten years old.

Is it OK to paint a garage door black?

Black works well on steel and timber doors when you use the right exterior paint. Dark colours absorb more heat, though, and on south-facing uPVC, GRP or roller doors that heat can cause expansion and stress. Some manufacturers restrict dark colours on certain doors for this reason. If you want a black door with no worries, choose one with a factory-applied black finish.

How often should a wooden garage door be repainted?

As a rough guide, a painted timber door usually needs recoating every three to five years, and stained finishes can need attention more often, especially on doors facing driving rain or strong sun. Check the bottom rail and end grain each year, and touch up any cracks or bare patches early. Following the manufacturer's recoat guidance helps protect both the timber and the warranty.
