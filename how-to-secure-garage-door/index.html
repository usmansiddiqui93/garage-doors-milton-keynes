How to Secure Garage Doors: Locks, Upgrades & Practical Tips

Practical ways to make your garage harder to break into, from extra locks and emergency release protection to choosing a more secure door.

The best way to work out how to secure garage doors is to tackle the weak points in order: fit additional locks (such as floor-mounted or side locks) to the main door, protect any emergency release on an electric door, upgrade or reinforce the garage side door, and add lighting or an alarm. If your door is old, damaged or a lightweight single-skin design, replacing it with a modern security-rated door is often the most effective fix. Garages are a common target because they hold bikes, tools and garden kit, and they can sometimes give access into the house.

This guide explains how garage doors lock, the most useful security upgrades for each door type, and when a new door is the better answer.

How to secure garage doors: why it matters

Thieves like garages for a few simple reasons: they're often out of sight of the house, contents are valuable and easy to sell, and older doors can be forced quietly. If your garage connects to the house through an internal door, a weak garage door can also be a route into your home. It's also worth knowing that many home insurance policies set conditions about locks on outbuildings and garages; our guide on whether home insurance covers garage doors explains what to check.

How do garage doors lock?

Different doors lock in different ways, so the right upgrades depend on what you've got.

  • Up and over doors usually have a central handle and lock cylinder linked by rods or cables to latches at each side of the frame.
  • Manual roller doors generally have a key-operated lock or locking bolts in the bottom slat or guides.
  • Electric roller and sectional doors are held shut by the motor and drive system. Many modern operators include anti-lift protection that resists the door being pushed up.
  • Side hinged doors often use a multi-point lock on the main leaf, with shoot bolts holding the second leaf into the frame and floor.

Security upgrades for up and over garage doors

Older up and over garage doors are among the easiest to attack. The single central lock relies on long rods or cables, and gaps around the frame can let someone lever or hook the mechanism. Useful upgrades include:

  • Replacement locks: worn handles and lock cylinders are common. Replacement locks for up and over garage doors are available for most makes, including Cardale, Henderson and Garador, and are a quick, low-cost fix.
  • Floor-mounted locks: a ground lock or garage door defender fixed into the drive in front of the door physically stops the door being lifted. This is one of the most effective additions for any up and over door.
  • Additional side locks: bolts or locks fitted inside at each side of the door hold the bottom corners down, which is where up and over doors are most often levered.
  • Internal hasp and padlock: if you can get in through a side or internal door, a strong hasp and staple with a closed-shackle padlock gives extra protection when you're away.
Safety note: Up and over doors are balanced by tensioned springs or cones. Don't drill or modify the door near springs, cables or arms, and never try to adjust spring tension yourself. If the door feels heavy or uneven, our spring and cable repairs team can put it right safely.

How to secure roller garage doors

A good-quality roller garage door is already a strong option, especially insulated aluminium or steel doors with a sturdy bottom slat and deep guide rails. To improve security further:

  • Fit ground locks or additional locks for roller garage doors that bolt the bottom slat into the floor or guides.
  • Check the guides are securely fixed and that slats aren't bent; damaged slats can be easier to force.
  • Ask about anti-lift or auto-locking devices for electric roller doors, which make the curtain harder to push up from the outside.
  • Make sure the manual override is inside the garage and not reachable through a gap.

Our guide are roller garage doors secure? goes into more detail on how roller doors compare with other types.

Electric garage doors and the emergency release

Do automatic garage doors lock? Yes, in the sense that the motor and drive hold them closed, and many modern operators have mechanical anti-lift features. The weak point on some sectional and up and over doors is the emergency release, usually a cord with a red handle hanging inside. On some installations an intruder can create a small gap at the top of the door and hook that cord. Ways to reduce the risk:

  • Fit a release shield or a lockable external emergency release, so the internal cord isn't the only way to disengage the motor.
  • Shorten or reposition the release cord so it can't be reached through a gap (while keeping it usable in an emergency).
  • Use a modern rolling-code remote, and keep remotes out of sight in your car and away from the front door.
  • Consider a keypad or app control with a PIN rather than leaving a spare remote in a car parked on the drive.

Side hinged and wooden garage doors

Wooden garage doors and side hinged doors are only as secure as their locks, hinges and frame. A multi-point lock, strong shoot bolts top and bottom on the second leaf, and hinge bolts that stop the doors being lifted off if the hinges are attacked all help. Check the timber around locks and hinges for rot, because soft wood makes even a good lock easy to force.

Don't forget the garage side door

The personnel door at the side or back of a garage is often the weakest point of all, especially an old timber door with a single basic lock. Upgrading to a composite or steel door with a multi-point lock tested to PAS 24 makes a big difference. Our garage side doors page covers the options. Treat any internal door from the garage into your house like an external door, with a proper lock.

Lighting, alarms and simple habits

  • Lighting: a dusk-to-dawn or PIR security light over the garage puts off opportunists.
  • Alarms: a battery-powered shed or garage alarm, or a sensor linked to your house alarm, is inexpensive.
  • Cameras: a visible camera or video doorbell covering the drive acts as a deterrent and records activity.
  • Cover windows: frosted film stops people seeing what's inside.
  • Lock up bikes and tools: a ground anchor inside the garage adds another layer.
  • Close the door: it sounds obvious, but leaving the door open while in the garden is a common way things go missing.

Security standards explained

When you look at security garage doors, you'll see a few standards mentioned. In plain terms:

  • LPS 1175 is a Loss Prevention Certification Board standard that tests doors and shutters against attack with set tools for set times, with higher ratings meaning tougher tests.
  • PAS 24 is a UK standard for the enhanced security of doors and windows, commonly used for personnel doors such as garage side doors.
  • Secured by Design is a police-backed initiative that recognises products meeting recognised security standards.

Some manufacturers, such as SWS with its SeceuroGlide range, offer roller doors with tested security ratings; see our SWS garage doors page for more.

When to replace rather than upgrade

Extra locks are worth it on a sound door, but they won't fix a door that's rusted, rotten, badly dented or a thin single-skin panel. If the door itself is the weak point, a new door is usually better value than adding upgrades. Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What are the best locks for garage doors?

For up and over doors, a floor-mounted ground lock or garage door defender is one of the most effective additions, alongside internal side locks at the bottom corners. For roller doors, bottom-slat locks or ground locks help. Side hinged doors benefit from multi-point locks and strong shoot bolts. The best choice depends on your door, so it's worth having it checked.

Can you replace the lock on an up and over garage door?

Yes. Replacement handles and lock cylinders are available for most up and over doors, including Cardale, Henderson and Garador models. It's usually a quick job. If the door's latches, rods or cables are worn as well, those can be replaced at the same time so the lock engages properly at both sides of the frame.

Do electric garage doors lock automatically?

Electric garage doors are held shut by the motor and drive when closed, and many modern operators include anti-lift protection that resists the door being forced upwards. The main thing to check is the emergency release cord inside, which on some doors can be reached through a gap. A release shield or lockable external release reduces that risk.

How do I secure my garage door from the inside?

If you have another way in and out, fit additional bolts or locks at each bottom corner of the door, or a hasp and staple with a closed-shackle padlock. On electric doors, protect or reposition the emergency release cord. Inside the garage, use a ground anchor for bikes and keep tools locked away so they can't be used to break into the house.

Is a new garage door more secure than adding locks?

If your existing door is in good condition, adding locks is cost-effective. But if it's rusted, rotten, dented or a thin single-skin panel, a modern door with a sturdy frame, good locking and anti-lift protection will be far more secure. We can assess your current door and give you honest advice either way.
