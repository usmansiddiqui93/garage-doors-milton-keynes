Side hinged garage doors in Milton Keynes are making a comeback. They open like a pair of large front doors, so there are no springs, cables or overhead tracks, and you can pop one leaf open to wheel out the bike or the lawnmower without opening the whole garage. We supply and fit side hinged doors in steel, GRP, timber and composite, for single and double garages across the city and the surrounding villages.

What is a side hinged garage door?

A side hinged door is made of two leaves hung on heavy-duty hinges in a frame fixed to the opening. The leaves open outwards onto the drive. They can be split equally down the middle, or unequally (often roughly one third and two thirds) so the smaller leaf can be bolted shut while the larger one works as an everyday entrance.

It's the oldest garage door design around, but modern versions are a big step up from the draughty timber doors many people remember. Today's doors come with insulated panels, weather seals all round, multi-point locks and factory-applied finishes.

Pros and cons of side hinged doors

The benefits

  • Full headroom and width. There's no gear inside the frame or under the ceiling, so you keep the full opening and your roof space.
  • Quick everyday access. Open one leaf to walk in rather than lifting the whole door.
  • Few moving parts. No tensioned springs or cables to wear out, so maintenance is simple.
  • Traditional appearance. Ideal for period houses, barn-style outbuildings and homes in conservation areas.
  • Great for converted or semi-converted garages. If the garage is a workshop or store, hinged doors with glazed panels feel more like a room than a garage.

The drawbacks

  • Swing space. You need clear space on the drive in front of the door for the leaves to open. A car parked close blocks them.
  • Wind. Open leaves need to be held back with stays or ground catches on breezy days.
  • Sloping drives. If the drive rises towards the garage, the leaves may catch. We check the gradient at survey.
  • Automation is possible but specialist. Arm-type operators work well, but cost more than a typical roller door motor.

Choosing materials and finishes

Steel side hinged doors

Side hinged metal garage doors are strong, slim and available in a broad range of colours, including on-trend anthracite and black. Insulated steel versions use a double-skin panel with a foam core. They're our most popular choice for modern homes. Find out more on our steel garage doors page.

GRP side hinged doors

GRP side hinged doors look like painted or stained timber but won't rot, warp or need repainting. They're a sensible middle ground if you love the look of wood but not the upkeep; see GRP garage doors.

Timber side hinged doors

Nothing beats real wood on a cottage or a character property. Hardwood and engineered timber doors can be stained or painted in almost any colour and made to measure for older, non-standard openings. Our wooden garage doors page covers timber choices and care.

Composite and uPVC

Composite and uPVC side hinged doors are low maintenance and can be colour-matched to your front door and windows for a coordinated look.

Glazing

Side hinged garage doors with windows let daylight in and suit workshops and garden rooms. Options range from small top-light panes to fully glazed upper sections with toughened or laminated glass.

Insulation, locks and automation

Insulated side hinged garage doors are a good option if your garage adjoins the house. Because the leaves close against a rebated frame with compression seals, they can be very draught-tight.

For security, look for a multi-point lock that throws bolts into the head and threshold of the frame as well as hooks into the second leaf. Ask about anti-snap cylinders and hinge bolts (security dogs) that stop the leaves being lifted off if the hinges are attacked.

Automatic side hinged garage doors use a pair of arm or ram operators, similar to those on electric driveway gates. They're well suited to wide double openings but do need enough clear space for the arms. We'll tell you at survey whether your opening suits automation.

Who are side hinged doors best for?

We often recommend side hinged doors for:

  • Older houses and cottages in places like Olney, Stony Stratford and Newport Pagnell, where a traditional look matters.
  • Garages used mainly for storage, bikes or as a workshop, where you mostly want to walk in rather than drive in.
  • Low or awkward openings with no headroom for a roller box or sectional springs.
  • Owners who want a matching side door or a door that can be easily swapped later for a conversion.

How we fit side hinged doors

Because the frame carries the whole weight of the leaves, getting it square, plumb and securely fixed is everything. At survey we measure the opening at several points, check the brick reveals and look at the drive levels in front of the door.

On the day, we remove the old door, fix the new frame into the reveals with heavy-duty fixings, hang the leaves, adjust the hinges so the gaps are even, fit the lock and the drop bolts on the fixed leaf and seal around the frame. A single door is usually done in around half a day. We'll also show you how to adjust the stays and keep the lock running freely.

Repairs and servicing for hinged doors

Side hinged doors are low-maintenance, but leaves can drop over the years, hinges wear and locks stiffen. We realign sagging leaves, replace hinges and lock mechanisms, renew seals and repair timber doors. If you're unsure whether to fix or replace an older door, our guide repair or replace your garage door? can help.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

How much space do I need in front of side hinged doors?

You need clear space on the drive at least as deep as the width of the widest leaf, plus a little extra. For an equal-split single door that's often around 1.1 to 1.3 metres. An unequal split reduces the swing of the everyday leaf. We check drive levels and parking space on the survey so the doors open freely.

Can side hinged garage doors open inwards?

Some models can be hung to open inwards, but it's less common because the leaves then take up space inside the garage and the frame and seals have to be designed differently. Outward opening is the standard for most side hinged garage doors as it keeps the garage fully usable. We can advise whether an inward-opening door is practical for your garage.

Are side hinged doors secure?

A modern side hinged door with a multi-point lock, drop bolts on the fixed leaf and hinge bolts is very secure, often more so than an older up and over door. The key is a solid frame fixed properly to the brickwork. For more ideas, see our guide on how to secure a garage door.

Do you fit side hinged doors to double garages?

Yes. Side hinged doors are available in widths that suit most double garages, typically up to around 4.8 metres depending on the material. Very wide leaves are heavy, so for big openings we may suggest a four-leaf folding arrangement or a sturdier steel frame. We'll recommend the best layout at survey stage.
