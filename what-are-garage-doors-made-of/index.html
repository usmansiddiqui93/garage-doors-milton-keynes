What Are Garage Doors Made Of?

Most UK garage doors are steel, but aluminium, timber, GRP and composite doors all have their place. Here is what each material is like to live with.

So, what are garage doors made of? In the UK, most garage doors are made of galvanised steel with a painted or coated finish. Insulated sectional doors use two steel skins with a polyurethane foam core, and most roller doors are made of foam-filled aluminium slats. The other common materials are timber (softwood or hardwood), GRP (glass reinforced plastic, or fibreglass) and composite or uPVC-faced doors. Each material has different strengths, maintenance needs and costs.

This guide explains each material, the parts that sit behind the door, and how to tell what your existing door is made of.

What are garage doors made of in the UK? The main materials

Steel garage doors

Steel is the most common garage door material in the UK. Doors are pressed from galvanised steel sheet (coated in zinc to resist rust) and then finished with a factory paint or plastic coating, sometimes with a woodgrain texture. Up and over doors are usually a single steel skin with a stiffened frame, while sectional doors use double-skinned steel panels with insulation in between.

  • Pros: strong, affordable, wide choice of styles and colours, low maintenance.
  • Cons: can dent, and scratches that go through to bare metal can rust if left untreated, particularly along the bottom edge.

So, what metal are garage doors made of? Usually galvanised steel; aluminium is the other common metal. See our steel garage doors page for styles and prices.

Aluminium garage doors

Are garage doors made of aluminium? Many are, especially roller doors. Aluminium is lighter than steel and does not rust, which suits doors that coil up and move every day. Roller door slats are typically roll-formed aluminium filled with insulating foam. Aluminium is also used for some contemporary sectional doors, often with large glazed sections, and for frames and trims.

  • Pros: light, corrosion-resistant, ideal for roller and modern glazed doors.
  • Cons: softer than steel, so it can dent, and premium aluminium doors cost more.

Read more about aluminium garage doors.

Timber garage doors

Timber is the traditional material and is still chosen for its looks, especially on period homes and in conservation areas. Timber doors can be softwood (such as treated redwood), cedar or hardwoods such as oak and iroko. Many quality timber doors use engineered timber, glued in layers, to reduce warping.

  • Pros: beautiful, natural, good insulator, easy to repaint or customise.
  • Cons: needs regular finishing, can swell or warp if neglected, usually heavier and more expensive.

Find out more on our wooden garage doors page.

GRP (fibreglass) garage doors

GRP, or glass reinforced plastic, is a moulded resin reinforced with glass fibres. It can be formed with a realistic woodgrain texture, giving the look of timber without the upkeep. GRP is often used for up and over and side hinged doors.

  • Pros: will not rust or rot, resists dents well, very low maintenance, lighter than timber.
  • Cons: can crack under a hard impact, and colours can fade slowly in strong sun over many years.

See GRP garage doors for more.

Composite and uPVC garage doors

Composite doors combine materials, typically a strong core (often foam or timber) faced with GRP, uPVC or steel skins. They are most common as side hinged doors and garage side doors, where they offer a solid feel, good insulation and very little maintenance. Read about composite garage doors.

What are roller garage doors made of?

Most domestic roller garage doors are made of interlocking aluminium slats, each a double-skinned profile filled with polyurethane foam for insulation and rigidity. The slats run in aluminium or steel guide rails with brush or rubber seals, coil around a steel barrel and sit inside an aluminium box above the opening. Heavier-duty roller shutters may use steel slats. If you want to know more about how all of this fits together, see our guide how do roller garage doors work?

What is inside an insulated garage door?

An insulated sectional door panel is a sandwich: two thin galvanised steel skins bonded to a core of rigid polyurethane foam. The foam gives the panel strength as well as insulation. Thicker panels generally insulate better. Seals are just as important as the panel: rubber or EPDM seals at the top, sides and bottom, and between panels, stop draughts and rain. Our guide are insulated garage doors worth it? explains when insulation is worth the extra cost.

The parts behind the door

Whatever the door panel is made of, the working parts are mostly metal:

  • Springs: high-tensile steel, either torsion or tension springs, sometimes zinc-plated or galvanised. They are under significant tension and should only be handled by a professional.
  • Cables: braided galvanised steel wire that lifts the door and transfers the spring force.
  • Tracks and frames: galvanised steel or aluminium.
  • Rollers and hinges: steel, often with nylon wheels for quieter running.
  • Seals: rubber, EPDM or brush strips.

Material and the environment

Where you live and how the door faces can influence the best material. A south-facing door takes the full force of the sun, which is harder on dark timber finishes and can slowly fade some coatings. Exposed, windy sites benefit from rigid, well-fixed doors and good seals. Doors close to busy roads or under trees pick up more grime, so a smooth steel or GRP finish that wipes clean is easier to live with. Whatever the material, a quick wash a couple of times a year and keeping the bottom seal clear of leaves and grit will help any door last longer.

How to tell what your garage door is made of

  1. Try a magnet. If it sticks, the door is steel. If not, it may be aluminium, GRP, timber or composite.
  2. Look at the inside. Steel up and over doors usually show a pressed panel with a frame and visible gear; timber shows grain and joints.
  3. Tap it. Single-skin steel rings slightly, GRP sounds duller, and timber or insulated panels sound solid.
  4. Check the bottom edge. Rust points to steel; soft or flaking wood points to timber that needs attention.
  5. Look for a label. Many doors have a manufacturer's label on the frame or inside the panel.

Which garage door material is best?

For most homes, steel offers the best balance of cost, strength and low maintenance. Aluminium is the go-to for roller doors and modern glazed designs. Timber suits period homes if you are happy to maintain it. GRP gives a timber look with minimal upkeep. If you plan to change the colour later, our guide can you paint garage doors? explains which materials take paint well. For a broader comparison of door styles, see types of garage doors, or browse all our garage door guides.

Tip: the material affects maintenance more than anything else. Steel and GRP need little more than a wash, while timber needs refinishing every few years to stay in good condition.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What metal are garage doors made of?

Most metal garage doors in the UK are made of galvanised steel, which is steel coated in zinc to resist rust, then painted or coated in the factory. Aluminium is the other common metal and is used for most roller door slats and some modern sectional doors. The springs, cables and tracks are usually steel.

Are garage doors made of aluminium?

Many are. Most domestic roller garage doors use foam-filled aluminium slats, because aluminium is light, flexible enough to coil and does not rust. Some contemporary sectional doors also use aluminium frames with glazed infill panels. Up and over and standard sectional doors are more often made of galvanised steel.

Which garage door material lasts the longest?

With reasonable care, steel, aluminium and GRP doors can all last for many years. Galvanised steel and GRP need very little maintenance, while timber can last a long time if it is refinished regularly but deteriorates faster if neglected. In practice, the springs and moving parts usually need attention well before the door panel wears out.

What are the best materials for an insulated garage door?

The best insulated garage doors use double-skinned steel or aluminium panels with a rigid polyurethane foam core, combined with good seals all round. Timber is a reasonable natural insulator, and composite doors with foam cores perform well too. Single-skin steel offers very little insulation, so it is not the best choice for an attached or heated garage.

Can you change the material of an existing garage door?

Not as such, but you can replace the door with one made of a different material, often in the same opening. For example, many people swap an old steel up and over for an insulated sectional, a roller door or a timber side hinged door. We survey the opening first to check size, headroom and the condition of the frame.
