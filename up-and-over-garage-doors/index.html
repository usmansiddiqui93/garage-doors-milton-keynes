Up and over garage doors are still the most familiar sight on Milton Keynes driveways. Many of the homes built across the city's 1970s, 80s and 90s estates were finished with one, and plenty of those doors are still the originals. We supply and fit new up and over garage doors in Milton Keynes and the surrounding towns, and we repair and automate existing ones too.

They're simple, tough and good value, and a new one can transform the front of a house for a modest outlay. Here's how to pick the right one.

Canopy or retractable? The two types of up and over door

Every up and over door is a single rigid panel that tilts up and slides back into the garage. How it gets there depends on the gear.

Canopy up and over doors

A canopy door pivots on arms fixed to the frame. When it's open, roughly a third of the door sticks out beyond the front of the garage, forming a small canopy over the drive. Canopy gear is compact, needs very little room inside the garage and is the most affordable choice. The trade-off is that you need clearance in front of the door while it swings, so you can't park tight against it.

Retractable up and over doors

A retractable door runs on horizontal tracks fixed to the garage ceiling, with tension springs to balance the weight. When open, the whole door slides back inside the garage, so nothing projects over the drive. Retractable gear is smoother, gives slightly more drive-through height, copes better with heavier timber and GRP panels and is generally the better choice if you want to add a motor later.

Quick rule of thumb: short driveway or plans to automate? Go retractable. Tight budget and a garage full of ceiling storage? Canopy usually wins.

Pros and cons of up and over doors

  • Value: a steel up and over door is usually the lowest-cost way to get a brand-new, good-looking garage door.
  • Choice of styles: dozens of panel designs, from vertical ribs and horizontal ribs to Georgian, cathedral and plain flush styles.
  • Familiar and robust: fewer moving parts than a sectional door, and many problems are quick to fix.
  • Retrofits easily: often fits straight into the existing frame size on older homes.
  • Clearance needed: the door swings out as it opens, so a car parked close will get in the way.
  • Reduced opening: the gear sits inside the frame, which slightly reduces the drive-through width and height.
  • Limited insulation: single-skin doors offer little thermal protection, though insulated versions exist.
  • Springs and cables: they wear out and need replacing every so often.

Materials, colours and finishes

Steel

Galvanised steel with a factory-applied paint finish is the most popular choice by far. It's light, strong and low maintenance. Most ranges come in white as standard with a choice of other colours and woodgrain effects at extra cost. Our page on steel garage doors explains coatings and how to keep them looking good.

GRP

Glass reinforced plastic up and over doors have a moulded woodgrain finish and won't rust or dent easily. They're heavier than steel, which is why they're normally hung on retractable gear. See GRP garage doors for the full picture.

Timber

Timber up and over doors suit traditional houses and conservation settings. They need periodic re-finishing but nothing beats real wood for character.

Insulation

Insulated up and over garage doors are available with a foam-backed or double-skinned panel. They won't match an insulated sectional, but they do take the edge off a cold integral garage.

Windows and personnel doors

Many designs can be ordered with small glazed panels along the top. Some ranges also offer an up and over door with a pedestrian door built into the panel, so you can walk in without lifting the whole door.

Locks, security and automation

A standard up and over door locks with a T-handle that drives bolts into the frame at either side. Over time, cheaper locks and worn bolts can become a weak point. Upgrades include multi-point locking, high-security cylinder locks and internal bolts. Fitting a motor also improves security, because the operator holds the door shut.

Electric up and over garage doors use a ceiling-mounted belt or chain operator. If you already have a sound retractable door, we can often automate it rather than replacing it; see our garage door automation service for details.

Who should choose an up and over door?

  • Homeowners who want a smart new door without the cost of a sectional or roller.
  • Landlords and sellers looking to lift kerb appeal before letting or selling.
  • Anyone with an existing up and over frame in good condition who wants a straightforward swap.
  • Owners of period-style homes who want a traditional timber or GRP look.

If you'd rather not lose space on the drive at all, a roller door or sectional door will suit you better.

Replacing an up and over door: the fitting process

Replacement up and over garage doors are usually a one-visit job. After we've surveyed and ordered the door, our fitters arrive, take out the old door and frame, check the opening is square, fix the new frame to the brickwork, hang the door and set the spring tension so it balances properly. Most single doors are finished in a few hours, and we take the old door away.

If the existing opening is out of square or the timber sub-frame has rotted, we'll point it out at survey stage rather than on the day, so there are no surprises on your bill.

Up and over door repairs

We repair all major makes, including Henderson up and over doors, Garador and Cardale doors, many of which have been on Milton Keynes houses for decades. Typical jobs include snapped springs, frayed cables, worn cones, broken locks and handles, and doors that have dropped on one side. Springs and cables are under tension and can cause serious injury, so please don't attempt to adjust them yourself; our garage door spring and cable repairs page explains what we do.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the difference between canopy and retractable up and over doors?

A canopy door pivots on arms and, when open, about a third of it sticks out over the driveway. A retractable door runs on ceiling-mounted tracks and slides fully inside the garage. Canopy doors are cheaper and take up less room inside; retractable doors are smoother, project less and are better suited to automation and heavier panels.

Can my existing up and over door be made electric?

Often, yes. Retractable doors in good condition are usually straightforward to automate with a ceiling-mounted operator. Canopy doors can be automated too, but they need a special arm kit and the door must be well balanced first. We check the door on survey and tell you honestly whether automating it or replacing it gives better value.

How long does an up and over door last?

A well-maintained steel up and over door commonly lasts 15 to 20 years or more, though springs and cables will usually need replacing along the way. Rust, dents and a sagging frame are the signs it's time to replace. Our guide on how long garage doors last covers the warning signs in more detail.

Will a new up and over door fit my existing opening?

In most cases, yes. Up and over doors are made in a wide range of standard sizes, and made-to-measure versions cover non-standard openings. We measure the brickwork on the survey and allow for the new frame. If the opening is unusual, we may recommend a timber sub-frame to make up the size neatly.
