GRP garage doors in Milton Keynes are the answer for homeowners who want the look of a painted or stained timber door without sanding, staining and repainting every few years. GRP stands for glass reinforced plastic, better known as fibreglass. It's moulded from real timber patterns, so the grain is surprisingly convincing, yet it won't rot, rust or swell. We supply and fit GRP up and over and side hinged doors, and we can automate and repair them too.

What is a GRP garage door made of?

A GRP door panel is made by laying glass fibre matting and resin into a mould, usually with a coloured gel coat on the outer face. Once cured, the panel is tough, rigid and weatherproof right through. Many GRP doors have a moulded skin bonded over a reinforcing frame, and some include a foam or board backing for extra stiffness and a little insulation.

Because the colour is in the gel coat rather than painted on top, light scratches don't expose bare metal or wood underneath. That's a big part of why GRP stays looking tidy for so long.

Pros and cons of fibreglass garage doors

Why choose GRP

  • Timber looks, minimal upkeep. The best way to get a traditional woodgrain door that won't need regular recoating.
  • Weatherproof. No rot, no rust, no swelling or shrinking in wet weather, so doors keep their shape.
  • Dent resistant. GRP flexes and springs back from minor knocks that would dent a steel door, such as a football.
  • Happy in shady spots. GRP copes well with north-facing or tree-shaded elevations where a timber door stays damp and deteriorates faster.

Things to consider

  • Weight. GRP panels are heavier than steel, so up and over doors are normally hung on retractable gear with springs matched to the weight.
  • Cracking. A hard impact, such as a car bumper, can crack GRP rather than dent it. Small cracks can sometimes be repaired; serious damage may mean a new panel.
  • Colour change over time. Gel coats can lose a little gloss or lighten slightly after many years of strong sun. A gentle clean and polish revives them.
  • Fewer modern styles. GRP is strongest in traditional designs; for flush contemporary doors, steel or aluminium usually offer more choice.

GRP door styles and options

GRP up and over doors

The most popular GRP format. Designs include vertical boarding, chevron, Georgian and cathedral-style panels, often in woodgrain colours like light oak, mahogany or teak-effect, or in white and painted shades. Our up and over garage doors page explains canopy and retractable gear.

GRP side hinged doors

Side hinged GRP doors are a great match for cottages and older homes, giving a traditional twin-leaf look with low maintenance. Read more about layouts and locking on our side hinged garage doors page.

GRP sectional doors

Less common, but some manufacturers offer sectional doors with a GRP or woodgrain-effect face. We can advise on availability at survey.

Colours and finishes

GRP doors come in woodgrain shades and plain colours. Can you paint fibreglass garage doors? Yes, with the right preparation: a thorough clean, a light key and a primer suited to GRP before your top coat. Painting does mean you'll need to maintain that paint, though. Our guide painting garage doors covers the steps.

Windows

Many GRP designs can be ordered with small glazed panels along the top for natural light in the garage.

Insulation, security and electric GRP doors

Insulation: GRP is a poorer conductor of heat than steel, and backed panels add a bit more, so a GRP door is a modest improvement over single-skin steel. If warmth is a priority, look at insulated sectional or side hinged doors instead.

Security: as with any door, security comes mainly from the frame, lock and gear. We fit GRP doors with good-quality locking and can add bolts or multi-point locks on side hinged models.

Automation: electric GRP garage doors are entirely possible. Because of the extra weight, we make sure the door is well balanced and fit an operator rated for it.

Who is a GRP door right for?

  • Owners who like the look of a wooden garage door but don't want the maintenance.
  • Traditional and period-style homes where a ribbed steel door would look out of place.
  • Busy family homes where footballs and bikes regularly hit the door.
  • Landlords who want a smart-looking door that stays smart between tenancies.

What fitting a GRP door involves

We start with a survey to measure the opening, check the brickwork and lintel and confirm the door will clear any ceiling lights or storage. Once ordered, the door is made to your size and finish.

On fitting day we remove the old door, install a new frame, hang the GRP panel or leaves and then carefully set the spring tension or hinge adjustment, because a heavier GRP door must be correctly balanced to run smoothly and safely. We'll show you how to clean the door, using mild soapy water and a soft brush and avoiding abrasive cleaners or pressure washers at close range.

GRP door repairs and servicing

We service and repair GRP doors from all major makes, including Cardale doors, plus replacing springs and cables, fixing worn gear and renewing locks. Springs on heavier GRP doors are under significant tension, so please leave adjustment to a professional. Our garage door servicing keeps everything balanced.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What does GRP stand for on a garage door?

GRP stands for glass reinforced plastic, which most people know as fibreglass. The door panel is moulded from glass fibre and resin with a coloured gel coat on the surface, often textured from real timber to give a woodgrain finish. It's tough, weatherproof and much lower maintenance than real wood.

How long do GRP garage doors last?

A good-quality GRP door commonly lasts 20 years or more because the material doesn't rot or rust. The springs, cables and locks will need servicing or replacing over time, just like on any up and over or side hinged door. Keeping the gel coat clean and the gear balanced helps it last as long as possible.

Can GRP garage doors be repaired if they crack?

Small cracks and chips in GRP can often be filled and colour-matched, although repairs may be visible close up. Larger cracks, especially near fixing points or across a whole panel, may mean the panel needs replacing. We'll inspect the damage and tell you whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

How do I clean a fibreglass garage door?

Wash it a few times a year with mild soapy water and a soft brush or sponge, then rinse with clean water. Avoid abrasive pads, strong solvents and close-range pressure washing, which can dull the gel coat. If the surface has lost its sheen, a non-abrasive polish or wax suited to GRP can help restore it.
