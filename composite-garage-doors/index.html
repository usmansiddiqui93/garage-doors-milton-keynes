Composite garage doors in Milton Keynes: the low-maintenance alternative to timber

Composite garage doors in Milton Keynes appeal to homeowners who love the traditional look of a painted wooden door but are tired of sanding, filling and repainting every few years. A composite door is made from layered materials — typically a reinforced frame, an insulating core and a tough outer skin such as GRP or a textured uPVC-type finish — bonded together so the door stays stiff, weatherproof and stable through hot summers and wet winters.

uPVC garage doors work on a similar idea with a slightly simpler build: multi-chambered PVC-U profiles, often steel-reinforced, with solid infill panels or glazing. Both are popular choices for side hinged garage doors and for the smaller pedestrian doors at the side or back of a garage. We cover both on this page because customers often ask for one and end up comparing it with the other.

Composite vs uPVC: what's the difference?

The two are related but not identical, and the right one depends on your budget and how the garage is used.

  • Composite doors tend to be thicker and heavier, with a solid or foam core. They feel more substantial, insulate well and usually come in a deeper woodgrain texture that's convincing up close. They cost more.
  • uPVC doors are lighter and generally cheaper. Modern foiled uPVC can look surprisingly good, and many people choose it to match existing uPVC windows and a front door. Panels are usually thinner than a composite slab.

Some manufacturers also offer aluminium composite garage doors, where aluminium skins sandwich an insulating core. These sit closer to our aluminium garage doors in look and price.

Styles available

Side hinged composite and uPVC doors

This is the most popular format. A pair of doors opens outwards, either as equal halves or a 2/3 and 1/3 split so you can nip in through the smaller leaf without opening both. Side hinged doors need no ceiling track or headroom, so they suit garages with low roofs, storage overhead or a workshop bench. Find out more about the format itself on our side hinged garage doors page.

Composite and uPVC up and over doors

A smaller number of ranges offer composite or uPVC-faced up and over doors, giving the familiar canopy or retractable action with a low-maintenance finish. They're less common than steel up and overs, so choice of design is narrower.

Pedestrian and personnel doors

uPVC and composite are ideal for the single door at the side or rear of a garage. We can match the colour and style to your main garage door or your house door. See garage side doors for sizes, locks and security options.

With or without windows

Top-light glazing, decorative panes or a full glazed upper panel are all possible. uPVC garage doors with windows are a common request where the garage doubles as a utility room or hobby space.

Advantages and drawbacks

What's good:

  • No painting, staining or treating — wipe clean with soapy water.
  • Won't rot, warp or swell like untreated timber.
  • Good insulation, particularly on solid-core composite doors.
  • Multi-point locking available on many side hinged and pedestrian doors.
  • Huge choice of colours and woodgrain foils, including grey composite and black.

What to weigh up:

  • A deep scratch can't simply be sanded and repainted the way timber can.
  • Very dark foils absorb more heat in direct sun; good manufacturers design for this, but it's worth discussing for south-facing doors.
  • Large uPVC doors can need steel reinforcement to stay rigid, which affects price.
  • Electric automation is less common on side hinged composite doors, though it's possible with the right operator.

Who are composite and uPVC garage doors for?

These doors make most sense if you want a traditional "cottage" or "carriage house" look without the upkeep, if the garage needs a door you can walk through easily, or if you want everything on the front of the house to match. They're a common pick on older semis in Bletchley and Wolverton where the original timber doors have finally given up, and on modern homes where the windows are already foiled uPVC.

If you want automation at the press of a button, a sectional door or roller door is usually the easier route. If you love real wood grain and don't mind maintenance, timber garage doors still have a character that's hard to beat.

Fitting your new door

We start with a survey to measure the opening and check the condition of the brick piers, lintel and floor. Side hinged doors are supplied in their own frame, so the frame needs a sound, square fixing all round. If your existing opening is uneven — common where an old up and over door has been ripped out — we'll explain any making-good needed before we quote.

On fitting day we remove the old door, fix the new frame, hang and adjust the leaves so they close evenly and seal properly, then fit the locks and any drop bolts. Most single openings are completed in a day. We'll take away your old door and leave the area tidy.

Good to know: keeping a composite or uPVC door looking its best is simple — clean it two or three times a year, lightly oil the hinges and locks, and check the seals. Our garage door maintenance guide has a quick checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Are composite garage doors better than uPVC?

Composite doors are generally thicker, sturdier and better insulated, with a more realistic woodgrain texture, so they feel more premium. uPVC doors cost less and can still look smart, especially in foiled colours. If budget is tight or you're matching existing uPVC windows, uPVC is often the sensible choice; if you want the most solid feel, choose composite.

Can composite or uPVC garage doors be electric?

It's possible on side hinged doors using a dedicated swing-door operator, but it's less common and depends on the door weight, the opening and how the leaves sit against the walls. If automation is high on your list, a sectional or roller door is usually simpler. We'll tell you honestly at the survey whether it's practical for your garage.

How long do uPVC garage doors last?

A well-made uPVC or composite garage door can last 20 years or more with basic care. The panels themselves rarely fail; it's usually hinges, locks or seals that need attention over time, and those parts can be adjusted or replaced. Regular cleaning and light lubrication of moving parts go a long way. Our lifespan guide explains more.

Do you supply uPVC garage doors with windows?

Yes. We can supply side hinged uPVC and composite garage doors with glazing in the top section, decorative glass, or larger glazed panels where you want more light in the garage. Obscure glass is available if you'd rather keep the contents private. Glazing does add to the price, so we'll show you the options side by side.

Can you replace just the side door on my garage?

Yes. We regularly replace pedestrian side and rear garage doors on their own, in uPVC, composite, steel or timber. It's a good opportunity to improve security with a multi-point lock and a stronger frame. We'll measure the opening and, if you like, match the colour to your existing garage door or house door.
