How to Maintain Garage Doors

A practical routine for keeping your garage door clean, quiet and reliable — including the best lubricant, what to avoid, and the jobs to leave to an engineer.

To maintain a garage door, keep it clean, lubricate the moving parts with a suitable silicone or lithium-based spray, check the seals, fixings and safety features every few months, and book a professional service every year or two. That simple routine prevents most of the common faults we are called out to, from noisy rollers to doors that stick halfway. What you should not do is touch the springs or cables: they are under high tension and dangerous to adjust without the right training and tools. This guide gives you a step-by-step routine you can follow safely.

Step 1: Look and listen

Open and close the door and pay attention. Healthy doors move smoothly and evenly. Warning signs include:

  • Grinding, squealing or banging.
  • One side lifting before the other.
  • A door that drops quickly, feels heavy, or will not stay open halfway.
  • Frayed cables, gaps in a spring coil, or loose fixings.
  • An electric door that hesitates, reverses for no reason or stops short.

If you spot any of these, stop using the door if it is unsafe and get it checked. Our garage door repairs team covers all major makes.

Safety first: springs and lifting cables hold the weight of the door under very high tension. If they snap or are released incorrectly, they can cause serious injury. Never try to adjust, remove or replace springs, cables or cable drums yourself. Call a professional — see our spring and cable repairs service.

Step 2: How to clean garage doors

Dirt, grit and road spray wear finishes and clog moving parts. A gentle clean a few times a year keeps the door looking good and running freely. Always use a soft brush or sponge, mild detergent and clean water, and rinse thoroughly. Avoid pressure washers at close range, abrasive pads and strong solvents.

How to clean metal garage doors

For steel and aluminium doors, wash with warm water and a mild detergent or car shampoo, working from the top down, then rinse and dry with a soft cloth. Pay special attention to the bottom edge, where salt and dirt collect. If you notice small chips, touch them up promptly to stop rust starting.

How to clean white metal garage doors

White doors show every mark. A non-abrasive cleaner or diluted car shampoo works well; for stubborn green algae, a dedicated exterior cleaner suitable for painted metal can help. Test any product on a small, hidden area first and never use bleach or scouring cream, which can dull or damage the factory finish.

How to clean wooden garage doors

Timber should be washed gently with mild soapy water and a soft brush, then left to dry. While cleaning, check for cracked or flaking paint, soft spots and bare wood, especially on the bottom rail. Recoat as needed — our guide to painting garage doors explains the right products.

How to clean fibreglass (GRP) garage doors

GRP doors need very little care. Wash with mild soap and water and a soft cloth. Avoid solvents and abrasives, which can scratch or dull the gelcoat surface.

Cleaning guides and tracks

Brush or vacuum leaves, cobwebs and grit out of the guide rails on roller doors and the tracks on sectional and retractable doors, then wipe them with a damp cloth. Clean tracks matter more than lubricated ones.

Step 3: Lubricate the right parts

What is the best lubricant for garage doors?

For most domestic garage doors, a silicone spray or a white lithium grease spray is the best choice. Both are designed to keep metal parts moving smoothly without attracting as much dirt as heavy grease. Some manufacturers supply or recommend their own lubricant, so check your door's instructions first.

  • Silicone spray: clean, dries quickly, good for hinges, rollers, locks and rubber seals.
  • Lithium grease spray: slightly heavier; good for metal-to-metal pivots, hinges and bearings.
  • Light machine oil: fine for some hinges and pivot points in small amounts.

What to avoid

  • WD-40 and similar water-displacing sprays are great for freeing a stuck part or cleaning, but they are not a long-lasting lubricant on their own.
  • Thick grease in tracks and guides collects grit and can make the door run worse.
  • Lubricating nylon rollers' running surfaces — lubricate the bearings or axle, not the rolling face.

Where to lubricate

  1. Hinges between panels on sectional doors.
  2. Roller bearings (the axle, not the wheel face).
  3. Pivot points and arms on up and over doors.
  4. Locks and handle mechanisms — use a lock lubricant or silicone, not oil.
  5. Rubber seals — a little silicone keeps them supple in winter.

Best lubricant for garage roller doors

Roller doors need very little lubrication. Keep the guide rails clean and, if the manufacturer allows, apply a light silicone spray to the guides. Do not grease them. Electric roller door motors are sealed and do not need lubricating.

Best lubricant for electric garage doors

The door hardware on an electric sectional or up and over door is lubricated the same way as a manual door. Many ceiling operators use a chain or belt; check the manual, as some chains benefit from light lubrication and many belt drives need none. Do not spray inside the motor housing.

Step 4: Check seals and fixings

Inspect the bottom rubber and side seals for splits, flattening or gaps. Worn seals let in draughts, rain and leaves, and on electric doors a damaged bottom seal can affect the safety edge. Seals are inexpensive to replace and make a big difference — read more in our guide to garage door weather seals. Also check that visible screws and brackets are tight, but leave anything connected to springs, cables or drums alone.

Step 5: Test electric door safety features

  • Obstruction test: place a sturdy object such as a piece of wood or a cardboard box on the floor under the door and close it. It should stop and reverse on contact.
  • Photocells: wipe the lenses, then wave something through the beam while the door is closing. It should stop or reverse.
  • Manual release: check you know where it is and how it works in a power cut.
  • Remotes: replace batteries when range drops.

If the door fails a safety test, stop using it automatically and get it checked. Our guide electric garage door not working? covers common faults.

When to book a professional service

Even with good DIY care, an engineer should look at the door every 12 to 24 months. A professional garage door service includes checking and adjusting the balance, inspecting springs and cables, tightening fixings, setting motor limits and force, testing safety devices and lubricating everything correctly. Regular servicing is also one of the best ways to extend your door's life, as explained in how long do garage doors last?

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the best lubricant for garage doors?

For most home garage doors, a silicone spray or white lithium grease spray is the best choice. Silicone suits hinges, rollers, locks and seals, while lithium grease is good for metal pivots and bearings. Avoid heavy grease in tracks and guides because it attracts grit. Check your door manufacturer's guidance too, as some recommend a specific product for their hardware.

Can I use WD-40 on my garage door?

Standard WD-40 is a water-displacing spray that is useful for freeing a stiff part or cleaning off grime, but it is not designed as a long-lasting lubricant. After using it, apply a proper silicone or lithium-based lubricant to hinges, rollers and pivots. Do not spray it into tracks or roller door guides, where it can mix with dirt and make things worse.

How often should I lubricate my garage door?

Every three to six months is about right for most homes, with an extra check before winter. Doors that are used many times a day or face harsh weather may need it more often. Use a small amount on hinges, roller bearings, pivots and locks, and wipe off any excess. Roller doors need very little lubrication; keeping the guides clean is more important.

How do I clean a metal garage door without damaging it?

Use warm water with a mild detergent or car shampoo and a soft brush or sponge, working from the top down, then rinse and dry. Avoid abrasive pads, scouring cream, bleach and strong solvents, which can damage the factory coating. Pay attention to the bottom edge where salt and grime collect, and touch up any chips promptly to stop rust spreading.

Can I adjust my garage door springs myself?

No. Garage door springs and cables are under high tension and can cause serious injury if they slip or break while being adjusted. This is a job for a trained engineer with the correct tools. If your door feels heavy, lifts unevenly or will not stay open, stop using it and book a professional check or repair.

Do electric garage doors need servicing?

Yes. The motor does not need lubricating, but the door hardware still wears, and the balance, limits, force settings and safety devices should be checked regularly. A door that is out of balance puts strain on the motor and can shorten its life. A professional service every 12 to 24 months keeps an electric door safe and reliable.
