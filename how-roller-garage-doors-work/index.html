How Do Roller Garage Doors Work?

A clear, jargon-free explanation of what happens when you press the button — plus how sectional, up and over and electric doors compare.

A roller garage door works by winding a curtain of horizontal slats (called laths) around a barrel mounted above the garage opening. When you press the remote, a motor inside the barrel turns it, the laths roll up neatly into a box at the top, and guide rails on each side keep the curtain straight. Closing reverses the process: the motor unwinds the curtain, which lowers under control until the bottom rail meets the floor, where the motor's brake holds it shut. Because the door rolls vertically into a compact coil, it takes up no space on your drive and almost none inside the garage.

The main parts of a roller garage door

Every roller door is built from the same basic components. Knowing what they are makes it much easier to understand quotes, choose options and describe a fault if something goes wrong.

The curtain and laths

The curtain is the part you see. It is made of interlocking laths, usually aluminium (sometimes steel), linked so they can flex around the barrel. Most domestic doors use double-skinned laths filled with foam insulation, which makes them stiffer, quieter and warmer. Laths come in different depths: smaller profiles roll into a smaller box, while deeper profiles are used on wider doors for extra strength.

The barrel (axle)

The barrel is a steel tube running across the top of the opening. The top lath is fixed to it with straps or brackets, so turning the barrel winds the curtain on or off.

The box (hood)

The rolled-up curtain sits in a box, usually made from aluminium, which protects it from weather and keeps the installation tidy. The box size depends on the door height and lath depth; for a typical single garage it is commonly somewhere around 250–300mm.

The guide rails

Upright guide rails on either side of the opening hold the edges of the curtain. They contain brush or rubber seals to reduce noise and draughts, and on better doors the laths have end locks that stop them being pulled out of the guides.

The bottom rail

The bottom slat is a heavier aluminium profile with a rubber seal underneath. It gives the curtain weight and rigidity and seals against the floor. On electric doors it often carries a safety edge.

The motor and controls

Most electric roller doors use a tubular motor that sits inside the barrel itself. It has a built-in brake and limit settings, which tell it where to stop at the top and bottom. Controls usually include handheld remotes, an internal wall switch, and sometimes a keypad or smartphone app.

Safety devices

Modern electric doors have obstruction protection. This might be a safety edge on the bottom rail that stops and reverses the door if it touches something, photocells that beam across the opening, or motor force detection. These help to protect people, pets and cars.

The manual override

So that you are never stuck in a power cut, every electric roller door has a manual override. Usually this is a winding handle inside the garage, which you hook into the motor and turn to raise the door. Where there is no other way into the garage, an external key-operated override can be fitted. Our guide electric garage door not working? covers what to check when the door will not move.

How do roller shutter garage doors work step by step?

  1. You press the remote or wall button. The control unit receives a coded signal and checks it.
  2. The motor releases its brake and turns the barrel.
  3. The curtain winds on to the barrel, the laths rolling neatly into the box while the guides keep them aligned.
  4. When the door reaches its upper limit, the motor stops and the brake holds it.
  5. To close, the motor runs in reverse. If the safety edge or photocells detect an obstruction, the door stops and reverses.
  6. At the lower limit the bottom rail sits on the floor and the motor holds the curtain shut, which also helps resist forced lifting.

Manual roller doors work the same way, except that a balanced spring inside the barrel does most of the lifting and you raise and lower the door by hand, locking it at the bottom.

How much space do roller garage doors need?

This is one of the big advantages of roller doors. They need:

  • Headroom for the box above the opening, commonly about 250–300mm on a single garage. If there is not enough room inside, the box can often be fitted on the outside face of the lintel.
  • Side room for the guide rails, typically a little under 100mm each side.
  • No ceiling space at all — the garage roof and loft area stay completely clear for storage or lighting.

Every installation is different, so we measure this carefully when we survey. Our guide to garage door sizes in the UK explains how openings are measured.

How do other garage doors work?

If you are comparing styles, here is how the main types of door operate.

How do sectional garage doors work?

A sectional door is made of four or five large insulated panels hinged together. Rollers on the panel edges run in tracks that go up the sides of the opening and then turn to run horizontally under the ceiling. Torsion springs on a shaft above the opening (or extension springs on some models) counterbalance the weight, so the motor or your hand only has to do a little work. The door lifts straight up and slides back under the roof. Read more on our sectional garage doors page.

How do up and over garage doors work?

An up and over door is one single panel that pivots out and up. On a canopy door, the panel swings out and part of it sticks out over the drive when open; it is usually balanced by springs at the sides of the frame. On a retractable door, the panel runs back fully into the garage on horizontal tracks, balanced by tension springs. Because the springs are under high tension, a broken spring or cable makes the door heavy or lopsided. These parts are dangerous and should only be dealt with by a professional — see our spring and cable repairs service.

How do side hinged garage doors work?

Side hinged doors open outwards like a pair of large front doors. They are the simplest mechanically, with hinges, a lock and bolts, and they can also be automated with arm-style motors.

How do electric and automatic garage doors work?

Whatever the door type, an electric garage door has the same building blocks: a motor, a control unit, a receiver for remotes, limit settings and safety devices.

  • Roller doors almost always use a tubular motor inside the barrel.
  • Sectional and up and over doors usually use a ceiling-mounted operator with a boom, chain or belt that pushes and pulls the door. Some sectional doors use a shaft-mounted motor instead.
  • Side hinged doors use arm motors mounted on the frame or inside wall.

Modern remotes send rolling or encrypted codes, so the code changes each time. Many systems can be linked to smartphone apps or smart home hubs. If a door is spring balanced, the motor is not lifting the full weight — it simply moves a door that the springs are already holding in balance, which is why a broken spring stops an electric door working. It is also possible to automate an existing garage door in many cases. For the full range, see our electric garage doors page.

Power cut tip: find out now where your manual override is and how it works, before you need it. If you have a roller door, check the winding handle is kept in the garage and not lost in a drawer.

Keeping a roller door working smoothly

Roller doors are low maintenance, but a little care goes a long way. Keep the guide rails clean and free of leaves and grit, avoid heavy grease in the guides (it attracts dirt), test the safety edge regularly, and change remote batteries when the range drops. Our garage door maintenance guide sets out a simple routine, and you will find more practical advice in our garage door guides.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

Can you open a roller garage door manually?

Yes. Electric roller doors have a manual override, usually a winding handle that hooks into the motor inside the garage. Turning it raises or lowers the curtain slowly. Where there is no other way into the garage, an external key override can be fitted. If you cannot find or operate your override, our engineers can show you how it works on your particular door.

Do roller garage doors have springs?

Manual roller doors usually have a spring inside the barrel that balances the curtain so it is light enough to lift by hand. Most electric roller doors do not rely on springs; the tubular motor does the lifting and holds the door with its brake. Spring-assisted doors should only be adjusted by a professional, because the spring is under tension.

How much headroom does a roller garage door need?

A typical single garage roller door needs roughly 250–300mm above the opening for the box, depending on the door height and lath size. Taller doors and deeper laths need a larger box. If there is not enough space inside, the box can often be mounted on the outside of the opening. We measure this carefully during a free survey.

How do electric roller doors know when to stop?

The motor has limit settings programmed during installation. These tell it exactly where the fully open and fully closed positions are, so it stops at the right point each time. If a door starts stopping short or overrunning, the limits may have drifted and need resetting. Safety devices also stop and reverse the door if it meets an obstruction.

Are roller garage doors noisy?

A well-fitted electric roller door is fairly quiet. Foam-filled laths, brush seals in the guides and a good tubular motor all reduce noise. Rattling or grinding usually points to dirty guides, a worn seal or a motor fault. If your door has become noticeably louder, a service is a sensible first step before the problem gets worse.
