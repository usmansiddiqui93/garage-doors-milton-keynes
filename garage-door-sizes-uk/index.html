Garage Door Sizes UK: Standard Sizes & How to Measure

Standard single and double garage door sizes, a clear sizes table, and how to measure your opening so your new door fits first time.

  • Simple measuring checklist

The most common garage door sizes UK homes have are 7ft x 6ft 6in (2134 x 1981mm) for an older single garage, with 7ft 6in, 8ft and 9ft wide singles (2286mm, 2438mm and 2743mm) common on newer homes, usually at 6ft 6in or 7ft (2134mm) high. Double garage doors are typically 14ft x 7ft (4267 x 2134mm), with 15ft and 16ft wide versions also widely available. Those are the starting point, but plenty of garages don't match them exactly, which is why measuring properly matters and why made to measure garage doors are so popular.

This guide covers the standard sizes, how to measure your opening, the headroom and side room each type of door needs, and when a made-to-measure door is the better choice.

Standard garage door sizes UK table

UK sizes are still usually quoted in feet and inches, because that's how most garages were built. Manufacturers list the metric equivalent, and you'll often see both on a quote. The table below shows the most common standard sizes.

Door size or opening size? With up and over doors in particular, the quoted size usually relates to the structural opening the door is made to fit, and the frame or subframe size can be slightly different. Different manufacturers word this differently, so always check what a quoted size actually refers to before ordering.

How wide are garage doors on new-build homes?

Newer homes tend to have wider single openings, often 7ft 6in to 8ft, partly because cars have got wider. Even so, many modern garages are still a tight fit for today's SUVs, so if you're replacing a door it's worth thinking about whether a door with fewer obstructions, such as a sectional or roller door, would give you more usable width.

How tall are garage doors?

The height of garage doors is most often 6ft 6in (1981mm) on older properties and 7ft (2134mm) on newer ones. Tall garage doors above 7ft are less common on houses but are made to order for campervans, vans and workshops, subject to the opening and the space inside.

Are garage doors a standard size?

Often, but not always. Lots of UK garages were built to standard sizes, yet over decades openings move, brickwork gets patched, and builders didn't always stick to the nominal figures. We regularly measure openings in Milton Keynes that are 20–40mm out from a standard size, or slightly out of square. A small difference can usually be dealt with using a subframe or timber packing; a bigger one is where a made-to-measure door comes in.

How to measure for a garage door

Measuring takes ten minutes with a tape measure and a notepad. Take every measurement in millimetres and write it down.

  1. Width: measure between the brick piers (the sides of the opening) at the top, middle and bottom. Use the smallest figure.
  2. Height: measure from the finished floor or driveway level to the underside of the lintel on the left, centre and right. Use the smallest figure, and note if the floor slopes.
  3. Headroom: measure from the underside of the lintel up to the ceiling or the lowest obstruction inside, such as a beam, pipe or light.
  4. Side room: measure the clear wall space on each side of the opening inside the garage, from the edge of the opening to the side wall or any obstruction.
  5. Depth: measure from the back of the opening into the garage to the first obstruction at ceiling height, such as a storage rack, boiler or loft hatch.

Also note anything that might get in the way: sockets near the opening, a consumer unit, a sloping ceiling, or a garage that steps down. These details affect which door will fit and how it's installed.

Tip: Your own measurements are great for getting a ballpark idea of prices, but we always re-measure on a free survey before anything is ordered. A made-to-measure door is built to our survey figures, so you're not left with a door that doesn't fit.

Every door type uses the space around the opening differently. The figures below are typical guides only, and they vary by manufacturer, model, spring system and whether a motor is fitted.

Up and over (retractable)A little more than canopySmall, within the frameRoughly the door height plus tracks

If you're short on headroom, a roller garage door fitted inside the opening or a low-headroom sectional may still work. If you've got plenty of depth but tight side room, a well-chosen up and over garage door is often the simplest option. For drives that slope towards the garage, side hinged garage doors can need special attention, as the leaves have to clear the ground when they swing out.

Fitting behind the opening or in the opening

Roller and sectional garage doors can usually be fitted either behind the opening (inside the garage) or within the opening itself. Fitting behind keeps the full clear width and height of the opening, but needs the headroom and side room listed above. Fitting within the opening needs no extra space but reduces the clear drive-through width and height a little. We'll talk you through the trade-off on site.

Made to measure garage doors

Most types of garage door can be made to measure. Roller doors are almost always manufactured to size, and sectional doors come in a huge range of sizes, often in small increments. Side hinged and timber doors are frequently made to measure, and made to measure wooden garage doors are a good way to fit an older or non-standard opening neatly. Up and over doors come in a wide range of standard sizes, with made to measure up and over garage doors available from some manufacturers for awkward openings.

Choose a made-to-measure door when:

  • your opening is well outside standard sizes, or out of square
  • you want a door to fit a wide or unusually tall opening
  • you want maximum clear width without bulky packing or subframes
  • you're matching a particular style, timber or finish

Made-to-measure doors tend to cost a little more and take longer to arrive, typically a few weeks depending on the manufacturer. Our bespoke and made-to-measure garage doors page covers the options in more detail.

Double garage door sizes and options

Double width garage doors are usually 14ft, 15ft or 16ft wide and 7ft tall. If you have a double garage you can fit one wide door or two single doors either side of a central pier. A single wide door gives easier access, but heavier wide doors need a strong lintel and properly specified springs or motors. See our double garage doors page for the types we fit.

Want to turn two single openings into one? Removing a central pier is structural work: it needs a correctly sized lintel or steel beam, and usually Building Regulations approval. Our garage conversion doors guide touches on this and on other ways to change a garage front.

Sizes, prices and installation

Size has a direct effect on price. A wider or taller door uses more material and often needs a stronger motor, while made-to-measure manufacture adds a premium. You can see typical figures on our garage door prices page, and our garage door installation service covers the survey, removal of your old door and fitting. Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the standard garage door size in the UK?

The most common standard single garage door size in the UK is 7ft x 6ft 6in (2134 x 1981mm), found on many older homes. Newer properties often have 7ft 6in, 8ft or 9ft wide singles at 6ft 6in or 7ft high. The standard double garage door is 14ft x 7ft (4267 x 2134mm), with 15ft and 16ft wide doubles also available.

How wide is a double garage door?

Most double garage doors in the UK are 14ft (4267mm) wide, with 15ft (4572mm) and 16ft (4877mm) versions common on larger garages. They're usually 7ft (2134mm) high. If your opening is a different width, roller, sectional and side hinged doors can be made to measure, so you don't have to stick with a standard double size.

Do I measure the opening or the old door?

Measure the opening, not the old door. Take the width between the brick piers and the height from the floor to the underside of the lintel, each in three places, and use the smallest figures. Then measure headroom, side room and depth inside. Old doors are often fitted with packing or subframes that hide the true opening size.

Can you get made to measure garage doors?

Yes. Roller doors are usually built to size, sectional doors come in a very wide range of sizes, and timber and side hinged doors are often made to measure. Some manufacturers also offer made-to-measure up and over doors. Expect a modest price premium and a lead time of a few weeks, depending on the manufacturer and finish.

What if I don't have much headroom in my garage?

If headroom is tight, a roller door fitted within the opening or a sectional door on a low-headroom track system are usually the best options. A canopy up and over door also needs very little headroom. We'll measure the space between the lintel and ceiling on a free survey and recommend doors that will genuinely fit.
