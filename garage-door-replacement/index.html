Replacement garage doors Milton Keynes homeowners choose

Many garages in Milton Keynes still have the door they were built with. Those original steel canopy doors have done well, but after decades of use they are often rusty, noisy, hard to lift and easy to force. Our replacement garage doors service in Milton Keynes takes care of the whole job: a free survey, help choosing the right door, removal and disposal of the old one, and a tidy fit of the new door.

We cover the whole borough and the towns around it, and regularly fit new garage doors in Bedford, Northampton, Aylesbury and Buckingham.

Signs it is time to replace your garage door

  • Rust has eaten through the bottom of the panel or the frame
  • The door has been hit by a car and the panel or frame is distorted
  • Timber is soft, split or rotten in several places
  • You have paid for several repairs in the last couple of years
  • The garage is cold, damp or draughty and you use it as a workshop, gym or utility space
  • Your door is easy to force and you keep bikes or tools inside
  • You simply want the front of the house to look better

Not sure? Our honest guide to whether you should repair or replace a garage door sets out where a repair still makes sense.

Like-for-like, or an upgrade?

Replacing an old up and over door with a new up and over is the simplest and lowest-cost option. Modern versions are lighter, better balanced and come with stronger locking than the door you are replacing. But a replacement is also the ideal moment to change door type, and many customers do.

Up and over to sectional

A sectional garage door rises vertically and sits under the ceiling, so you can park right up to it on a short driveway. Most are insulated and electric as standard. You need enough headroom above the opening for the tracks, which we measure at the survey.

Up and over to roller

Roller doors coil into a compact box above the opening, leaving the ceiling completely clear for storage or lighting. They are a good fit for garages with little headroom or low beams.

Upgrading to insulated

If you heat the house through an internal door or use the garage as a room, an insulated garage door makes a real difference to comfort and condensation.

Choosing materials and colours

Steel remains the most popular material for replacement doors because it is strong, affordable and available in many colours and woodgrain effects. GRP gives a convincing timber look without the upkeep, and timber itself suits period homes and village properties around Olney, Woburn Sands and Winslow, where a painted or stained wooden door can look far better than a plain steel one. Aluminium and insulated steel are favourites for contemporary homes, often in anthracite grey or black to match new windows. At the survey we bring samples so you can see colours against your own brickwork, which is far more reliable than judging from a screen.

A chance to improve security

Older canopy doors often rely on a single central lock that can be bypassed by bending the corner of the panel. New doors use multi-point locking, anti-lift features and stronger frames. If your garage has an internal door into the house, or you store bikes and tools there, a replacement is the most effective security upgrade you can make. Ask us about doors tested to recognised security standards if that is a priority.

How our replacement service works

  1. Free survey. We visit, measure the opening, headroom, side room and depth, check the brickwork and power supply, and talk through how you use the garage.

Replacing a garage door with French doors or a window

If you are turning the garage into a room, the door is often replaced with French doors, a window and brick infill, or a combination. That kind of change usually needs building work and may need Building Regulations approval, so it is a different project from a straight door swap. Our guide to garage conversion doors explains the options and what to check first.

What about replacement panels or windows?

For some modern doors, a single damaged section or a cracked vision panel can be replaced on its own. Whether that is possible depends on the make, age and colour, because panels must match exactly and some ranges are discontinued. Replacement panels for older canopy doors are rarely available. We will check for you, but be prepared that a full replacement is sometimes the better-value route.

How much do replacement garage doors cost?

The cost of new garage doors depends mainly on the type, material, size and whether you want electric operation. A manual steel up and over door is the most affordable replacement; an insulated electric sectional door in a custom colour sits at the higher end. See detailed guide ranges on our garage door prices page. Your actual price is confirmed in writing after the free survey.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

How long does it take to replace a garage door?

Most single replacements are completed in a day, including removing the old door and frame. Double doors, electric doors that need wiring commissioned, or openings that need building work can take longer. The bigger wait is usually manufacturing, as most new doors are made to measure, and we give you a realistic lead time with your quote.

Do you take away the old garage door?

Yes. Removal and disposal of your existing door and frame is part of our replacement service and will be shown on your quote. We release old springs safely before taking the door apart, and we leave the garage clean and tidy when we finish.

Can I replace an up and over door with a roller or sectional door?

In most cases, yes. Roller and sectional doors fit the same openings as up and over doors, but each has its own space requirements. Sectional doors need headroom and side room for the tracks, and roller doors need space for the box above the opening. We measure everything at the free survey and tell you which options will work.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace my garage door?

A repair is almost always cheaper in the short term. Replacement becomes better value when the door is badly rusted, rotten or distorted, or when you are paying for repeated repairs. We can price both options so you can compare. Our repair or replace guide explains the decision in more detail.
