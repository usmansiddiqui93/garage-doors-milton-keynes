This page brings together all the types of garage doors in Milton Keynes that we supply and fit. Choosing a new garage door comes down to four questions: how the door opens, what it is made of, how you want to operate it, and how it should look. Below we explain how to narrow things down, and you will find every door page listed further down.

Start with how the door opens

The opening mechanism matters most, because it decides how much space the door needs inside and outside the garage.

  • Up and over doors — one panel that tilts up and back. The most affordable option and the most common door on homes across the UK. Canopy versions stick out onto the drive slightly as they open.
  • Roller doors — slats that coil into a box above the opening. Nothing swings out and the ceiling stays clear, so they suit short driveways and garages used for storage.
  • Sectional doors — insulated panels that rise vertically and run back under the ceiling. Excellent sealing and a smart, modern look.
  • Side hinged doors — a pair of doors that open outwards. Good for frequent pedestrian access and traditional properties.

If you would like these compared in more depth, our guide to types of garage doors in the UK covers the pros and cons of each.

Then choose a material

Most door types come in several materials. Steel is strong and good value. Timber gives warmth and character but needs periodic treatment. GRP (glass reinforced plastic) looks like painted wood without the upkeep. Aluminium is light and resists corrosion, which is why it is used in most roller doors. Composite and uPVC are popular for pedestrian side doors. Our guide to what garage doors are made of goes into the detail.

Decide on manual or electric

New garage doors are increasingly sold with a motor. Roller and sectional doors are usually electric as standard, and up and over and side hinged doors can have one added. Electric garage doors are easier to use, especially if you drive in and out every day, and lock automatically when closed.

Think about insulation and security

If your garage is integral to the house or you use it as a workshop, gym or utility area, an insulated garage door will make it noticeably warmer and quieter. If you keep bikes, tools or a car in there, look at our options for secure garage doors, including doors tested to recognised security standards.

Standard sizes cover most garages, but we also supply made-to-measure doors for older or unusual openings, and wide doors for double garages. Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most.

What is the most popular type of garage door in the UK?

Up and over doors are still the most common on existing UK homes because they have been fitted as standard on houses for decades and are affordable. For new installations, roller and sectional doors have become very popular because they are usually insulated, electrically operated and save space on the drive. The best choice depends on your garage, driveway and budget.

Which garage door type takes up the least space?

Roller garage doors take up the least space overall. They coil into a box above the opening, so they do not swing out onto the drive or use up ceiling space inside. Sectional doors also open vertically without swinging out, but they run back along the ceiling, so they need clear headroom and depth inside the garage.

Can I buy garage doors for sale online and have you fit them?

We generally supply and fit our own doors, because we can then measure accurately, make sure the right door is ordered and provide guarantee details covering both the door and the installation. If you have already bought a door, get in touch with the details and we will let you know whether we can help.

Do new garage doors need planning permission?

Replacing a garage door like for like does not usually need planning permission. However, if your property is listed, in a conservation area, or subject to restrictive covenants, or if you are changing the opening, you should check with your local council first. We can advise on the practical side during your free survey.

Which garage door gives me the most room on the drive?

Roller and sectional doors open straight upwards and don't swing out, so you can park right up to the garage. Up and over doors swing out at the bottom as they open, and side hinged doors need room for the leaves to open outwards.
